# Cross-cutting synthesis

Terminology lock, restated for this file per Section 4 of RESEARCH-BRIEF.md: **HMF** = human/breast milk fortifier generically. **BMF/BMBF** is reserved exclusively for *bovine* milk-based fortifier, used only when quoting a paper (N-forte/Jensen, Ananthan 2020, Galiș 2024) that defines it that way as the explicit comparator to HMBF (human-milk-based fortifier). Where "BMF" appears in a paper title in this brief's own colloquial sense — meaning breast milk fortifier generically — as in Marino 2018 (PMID 30552093), this is flagged explicitly in that paper's `bmf_terminology_check` extraction field and is not conflated with the bovine sense used elsewhere.

## Decision-relevant table

## Statement of what is extrapolated versus directly evidenced

Two clinical scenarios in this brief rest substantially or entirely on extrapolation rather than direct trial evidence, and this must be stated to any reader without hedging:

1. **AEDF/REDF (Domain 2).** Every element of current practice for this subgroup — delaying feed initiation, and by convention also delaying fortification, until clinical stability — is extrapolated from (a) a feed-*initiation* RCT (ADEPT) that did not test fortification at all, and (b) small, methodologically dated epidemiological studies (Malcolm 1991, Wilson 1991) establishing the AREDF-NEC mechanistic link but containing no fortification data whatsoever. No RCT of fortification timing, dose, or safety has ever been conducted specifically in this subgroup. This is, on the evidence retrieved for this brief, the single largest evidence-to-practice gap in the entire topic.

2. **Post-surgical NEC/SIP fortifier reintroduction (Domain 3, sub-questions ii and iii).** Unlike Domain 2, where an adjacent (if indirect) trial tradition at least exists, Domain 3's fortifier-specific sub-questions have essentially no evidence base to extrapolate *from* at all — even the two most current, most authoritative guideline documents in this exact clinical area (ERNICA 2024, Italian SIN/SICP/SINUPE 2026) are silent on fortifier-specific timing, despite both addressing general post-surgical enteral nutrition rigorously. Whatever a given unit's local practice is for reintroducing fortifier after surgical NEC or SIP, it does not currently rest on any identified published trial, cohort, or even expert-consensus guideline statement — it is unit-level clinical judgement, full stop, and should be described as such.

By contrast, Domains 1, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8's general-VLBW findings, while frequently of low or very-low GRADE certainty, are at least **direct** — they derive from trials and reviews that actually randomised or observed a fortification-related variable in a broadly relevant population, even where that evidence is thin, inconsistent, or null.

The pattern across this ledger is consistent with the brief's prediction: this is a field with several underpowered positive single-centre or QI reports (Marino 2018, Lin 2020) sitting alongside a smaller number of larger, more rigorous trials and reviews that are disproportionately null or that reveal an unresolved trade-off (N-forte, Ananthan, Bell, Klamer, Zachariassen, Hopperton/O'Connor). Any manuscript drawn from this brief should lead with this pattern rather than defaulting to the more numerous positive single-centre reports.

## What would change practice

Ranked by how directly each identified trial or gap, if resolved, would change current recommendations:

1. **A fortification-timing RCT specifically in the AEDF/REDF or Doppler-abnormal growth-restricted subgroup.** No such trial exists. Given that this is the single largest extrapolation gap identified (Domain 2), an adequately powered trial here — even a modest pilot — would immediately upgrade current practice from "extrapolated caution" to "evidence-based guidance" for a subgroup where clinicians are already making cautious decisions without direct evidence.

2. **N-forte's planned 2- and 5.5-year neurodevelopmental follow-up** (Jensen et al., protocol PMID 34815288; short-term results PMID 38545091, not yet extended to ND). This is the largest, most rigorously designed fortifier-source RCT in extremely preterm infants; its long-term ND readout, whenever published, will be the first genuinely adequately-powered answer to whether HMBF vs BMBF affects neurodevelopment, and could either resolve or deepen the tension between the positive meta-analyses (Ananthan, Grace, Galiș) and N-forte's own null short-term composite outcome.

3. **The MaxiMoM InForM trial** (Beggs et al. 2025, protocol PMID 41047263, planned n=615, NCT05308134) — a three-arm RCT of standard vs target vs BUN-adjustable fortification with Bayley-IV cognitive score at 18–24 months as its primary outcome. This is the first adequately powered trial with a hard long-term ND endpoint for the targeted/individualised fortification question (Domain 8), and its results would directly address the uncertainty Bell et al.'s 2026 meta-analysis explicitly flags as unresolved.

4. **A replication of the Schanler acidified-vs-non-acidified safety signal.** A single multicentre RCT (n=164) currently underlies a clear, clinically actionable metabolic-acidosis signal; independent replication would convert a moderate-certainty single-trial finding into a more robust basis for product-selection guidance.

5. **Any prospective trial (rather than historical-control cohort) of fortifier-reintroduction timing after surgical NEC/SIP.** Given that even the current international guideline (ERNICA) and position paper (SIN/SICP/SINUPE) are silent on this question, even an observational multicentre registry study (e.g., via national neonatal audit infrastructure) characterising current practice variation would represent meaningful progress from the current near-total absence of data.
