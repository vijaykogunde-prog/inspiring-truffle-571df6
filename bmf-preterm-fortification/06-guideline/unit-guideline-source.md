**How to use this document.** Every recommendation below carries a certainty label taken directly from the underlying evidence review — High / Moderate / Low / Very low certainty (GRADE), or **GPP** (Good Practice Point) where no direct trial evidence exists in that exact clinical scenario and the statement instead reflects extrapolation from adjacent evidence and clinical consensus. This distinction is deliberately visible throughout: several of the areas where practice is most established (fortification in Doppler-abnormal growth restriction; fortifier reintroduction after surgical NEC/SIP) are GPP, not evidence-based, and this guideline says so rather than implying otherwise.

## 1. Scope and purpose

This guideline covers the timing, dose, product selection, tolerance monitoring, and post-discharge management of human milk fortification for preterm infants fed mother's own milk and/or donor human milk. It applies to infants cared for on this neonatal unit from admission until discharge, and includes discharge-planning guidance for continuing or stopping fortification at home.

**Out of scope:** parenteral nutrition; nutrient-enriched *formula* for formula-fed infants (a different intervention — see Section 2); general trophic/minimal-enteral-feeding protocols not specific to fortification; term infant feeding.

## 2. Definitions

- **HMF (human/breast milk fortifier):** the generic term used throughout this guideline for any product added to human milk to increase its nutrient density.
- **BMF / BMBF (bovine milk-based fortifier):** reserved specifically for fortifier derived from cow's milk, used only when distinguishing it from human-milk-derived product (HMBF). Do not use "BMF" to mean human milk fortifier generically — this exact confusion exists in the published literature and this guideline avoids it deliberately.
- **HMBF (human milk-based fortifier):** fortifier derived from pooled donor human milk rather than bovine milk.
- **AEDF/REDF:** antenatal absent or reversed end-diastolic flow on umbilical artery Doppler — a marker of placental insufficiency, distinct from birthweight-centile-defined IUGR/SGA.
- **GPP (Good Practice Point):** a recommendation based on clinical consensus and extrapolation from adjacent evidence, used explicitly wherever no direct trial evidence exists for the exact scenario in question.

## 3. Summary algorithm

**See Figure 1 (`figures/algorithm.png`).** All infants should be triaged through this algorithm before starting or restarting fortification: first for antenatal Doppler abnormality/growth restriction (Pathway A), then for a current or recent NEC/SIP episode (Pathways B and C for surgical and medical NEC respectively). Infants meeting neither criterion follow the routine pathway (Pathway D → Box 1 below).

## 4. Box 1 — Routine fortification checklist (Pathway D)

For infants with **no** antenatal AEDF/REDF or growth-restriction history and **no** current/recent NEC or SIP:

1. **Timing.** Commence HMF once enteral feeds reach the unit's standard threshold (commonly ~100 mL/kg/day). Starting earlier (in the 20–40 mL/kg/day range) has not shown a consistent growth or tolerance advantage in trials, and one meta-analysis found early fortification associated with a **longer hospital stay** with no growth benefit. *[Low–Very low certainty; do not adopt very-early fortification as a routine unit policy on current evidence.]*
2. **Introduction.** Introduce fortifier gradually rather than at full strength immediately. Observational data link rapid/early/full-strength introduction to higher feeding-intolerance risk. *[Very low certainty, but low-cost and low-risk to implement.]*
3. **Product — source.** Standard bovine-derived HMF remains the default. Whether human-milk-derived fortifier (HMBF) reduces NEC or mortality compared with bovine fortifier is **unresolved**: three meta-analyses suggest benefit, but the largest and most rigorous single RCT (N-forte) found no difference, and the meta-analyses' own NEC signal loses significance when high-risk-of-bias trials are excluded. Do not present HMBF to families as an established NEC-prevention strategy. *[Low–Moderate certainty, internally inconsistent — see Figure 2.]*
4. **Product — chemistry.** Where a non-acidified liquid fortifier alternative is available, prefer it over an acidified liquid product: one adequately sized RCT found substantially more metabolic acidosis (27% vs 5%) and poorer early feed tolerance with the acidified product. *[Moderate certainty.]*
5. **Individualised/targeted fortification.** Do not improvise a modular (ingredient-by-ingredient) targeted-fortification protocol locally. Pooled evidence shows a real growth benefit from individualised fortification, but one RCT attempting a modular approach was **stopped early after serious feeding intolerance in 5 of 39 infants**. Await a completed adequately powered trial (Section 9) before adopting targeted fortification as routine practice. *[Growth: Moderate certainty. Safety/feasibility of modular titration: demonstrated failure in one RCT.]*
6. **Tolerance monitoring.** Monitor stool pattern, gastric residual volume, and abdominal distension as fortification is introduced and advanced; document these explicitly rather than relying on a single "feeding intolerance" label. Stool hardening scales with fortifier dose regardless of product. *[Low certainty.]*
7. **Osmolality.** Keep total feed osmolality — including any co-administered medications, which independently raise osmolality — near the consensus ~450 mOsm/kg ceiling. This is an expert-consensus safety ceiling, **not a threshold validated against hard clinical outcomes in any randomised trial**; treat it as precautionary, not as evidence-based proof of a safe cut-off.
8. **Neurodevelopmental counselling.** When discussing fortification with families, do not imply a neurodevelopmental benefit. No randomised trial of any fortification variable (whether to fortify, fortifier source, or individualisation strategy) has yet shown a clear neurodevelopmental benefit. *[Moderate certainty for this null, from two independent RCT follow-ups.]*
9. **Discharge planning.** Growth and 6-year IQ outcomes do not differ between infants discharged on fortified versus unfortified mother's milk in the one RCT with long follow-up. If a family wishes to continue home fortification, frame the discussion around **supporting continued breastfeeding/human-milk feeding** (where continuing HMF, rather than switching to formula-based enrichment, is associated with higher continued human-milk-feeding rates) rather than around an expected growth or developmental benefit, neither of which is supported by current evidence. *[Low–Moderate certainty, consistently null on growth/neurodevelopment.]*

## 5. Pathway A — Antenatal AEDF/REDF or Doppler-abnormal fetal growth restriction

**No fortification-specific trial has ever been conducted in this subgroup.** Current practice of delaying fortification until full established feeds and clinical stability is an extrapolation from a feed-*initiation* trial (ADEPT) and from small, decades-old epidemiological studies establishing the AEDF–NEC association — neither tested fortification. A subgroup analysis of the same feed-initiation trial found infants below 29 weeks tolerated an identical feeding protocol far worse than infants ≥29 weeks (39% vs 10% NEC), warning against applying one timing rule uniformly across this population even for feed initiation, let alone fortification.

**Recommendation:** manage feed initiation per existing unit protocol for growth-restricted/Doppler-abnormal infants; introduce fortification only once feeds are fully established and the infant is clinically stable, individualised by gestational age and clinical trajectory. *[GPP — no direct evidence; state this explicitly to trainees and, where relevant, to families.]*

## 6. Pathways B and C — Necrotising enterocolitis and spontaneous intestinal perforation

**Pathway C (medical NEC):** enteral feeds may reasonably be restarted once portal-venous gas has been absent on ultrasound for approximately three consecutive days, per the one cohort study addressing this directly — though that study's own authors state it was underpowered to exclude an increased recurrence risk. *[Very low certainty.]* **Fortifier-specific reintroduction timing after feeds restart is not addressed in any identified trial** — apply clinical judgement, individualised to the infant's tolerance of unfortified feeds first. *[GPP.]*

**Pathway B (surgical NEC/SIP):** restart feeds per unit surgical/GI protocol. **No trial, and no current international guideline** (the 2024 European ERNICA surgical NEC guideline and the 2026 Italian SIN/SICP/SINUPE joint position paper were both checked specifically and both are silent on fortifier-specific timing) addresses when to reintroduce fortifier after surgical NEC or SIP. Whatever this unit's local practice is here, it should be understood and documented as clinical judgement, not as an evidence-based or guideline-based protocol. *[GPP — evidence absent, not merely extrapolated.]*

## 8. Monitoring, escalation and audit

- Any infant developing signs of feeding intolerance, abdominal distension, or suspected NEC/SIP during fortification should have fortification held and escalated per the unit's existing NEC/sepsis escalation pathway — this guideline does not change that pathway.
- Suggested local audit measures: proportion of eligible VLBW infants fortified by 100 mL/kg/day feeds; documented fortifier-hold events and their resolution; growth (weight/length/HC z-score change) at discharge; proportion of infants discharged on continued human-milk feeding, fortified or not.
- Any local deviation from Pathway A or Pathway B/C recommendations (both GPP) should be documented with rationale, given the absence of direct trial evidence to fall back on.

## 9. What could change this guideline

Named trials worth tracking for the next review:

1. An adequately powered RCT of fortification timing specifically in AEDF/REDF or Doppler-abnormal growth-restricted infants (none currently registered, so far as this review identified — the largest single gap in the evidence base).
2. The N-forte trial's planned 2- and 5.5-year neurodevelopmental follow-up (human-milk-based vs bovine-based fortifier, extremely preterm infants) — not yet published as of this guideline's evidence base (2026-09).
3. The MaxiMoM InForM trial (standard vs target vs BUN-adjustable fortification, n=615 planned, Bayley-IV cognitive score at 18–24 months as primary outcome) — the first adequately powered trial with a hard long-term endpoint for individualised fortification.
4. Independent replication of the acidified-vs-non-acidified metabolic-acidosis signal (Section 4, point 4), currently resting on one trial.

## 10. Roles and responsibilities

- **Bedside nursing and medical staff:** follow the algorithm (Figure 1) and Box 1 checklist for all eligible infants; document tolerance monitoring; escalate per Section 8.
- **Consultant/nutrition lead:** approve any deviation from Pathway A/B/C GPP recommendations; review audit data annually.
- **Pharmacy:** support osmolality-aware medication administration practice per Section 4, point 7.
- **Guideline author/owner:** review this document against Section 9's named trials, or at the stated review date, whichever is sooner.
