# Domain C — Other alloantibodies (Kell, anti-c, anti-E, mixed)

Search: PubMed, 20 Sep 2026 (full string in `01-search-log/searches-CD.md`, search C1). 36 records on Kell/anti-c/anti-E/IVIG/neonate; after full title-abstract-MeSH screening, 15 records included (14 case reports/case series plus 1 retrospective cohort). Extraction rows C1–C15 in `02-extraction/extraction-C.csv`. Mechanistic background specific to Kell disease is set out in `03-synthesis/00-mechanism.md` and is cross-referenced, not repeated, below.

## (a) What the evidence shows, and in whom

**No randomised controlled trial, quasi-RCT, or comparative cohort of IVIG for Kell, anti-c, anti-E or mixed non-D alloimmune HDFN was identified.** This is the central finding of Domain C and is reported as such per CLAUDE.md rule 10 ("absence is a finding"), not as a gap to be filled by extrapolation. The entire identified evidence base is 13 single-patient case reports, one two-patient case series (Usman 2013, C8, PMID 23559775), and one retrospective descriptive cohort (Healsmith 2019, C15, PMID 30723901) — Level 4–5 evidence by any standard hierarchy, and Very low certainty by GRADE for every row in the extraction table, by design rather than by any specific flaw.

Within this case-report base, the antibody types identified are: Kell-system antibodies (anti-k/Cellano, C1, PMID 41930090; anti-Ku/K0 phenotype, C6, PMID 31948214); isolated non-D Rh antibodies (anti-c, C3 and C14, PMID 36344387 and 15332750; anti-E, C8 and C9, PMID 23559775 and 20092394; anti-e, C5, PMID 34170643; anti-Ce, C10, PMID 10827261); and mixed/multi-antibody presentations (anti-D+anti-C, C2, PMID 37013314; anti-D+anti-C+anti-E, C4, PMID 35944885; anti-E+anti-c, C12, PMID 1709770; Rh(D)+(C)+Kell, C11, PMID 8580635; anti-D+anti-C+anti-Fya+anti-M, C13, PMID 42570441). One further case (anti-SARA, a rare low-frequency antigen outside Rh/Kell entirely, C7, PMID 28854515) illustrates that "other alloantibodies" extends beyond the brief's named examples. All are postnatal-IVIG cases (antenatal-only maternal IVIG cases were excluded on the same postnatal-administration criterion used in Domain A — see `01-search-log/searches-CD.md` for the full exclusion list, including a Kell-specific antenatal case, PMID 41170822).

The one cohort identified, Healsmith et al. 2019 (C15, Acta Obstet Gynecol Scand, PMID 30723901), is a 5-year retrospective review of 115 non-D Rh alloimmunised pregnancies (102 mothers) at a single Australian tertiary centre. It reports that 10 of the 115-pregnancy cohort's neonates received IVIG, alongside 59 who received phototherapy and 9 treated for anaemia — but it does not disaggregate the IVIG-treated group by antibody subtype, does not report an exchange-transfusion or bilirubin outcome specific to the IVIG group, and has no comparator (non-IVIG) group reported against which to judge effect. It therefore establishes only that postnatal IVIG is used in practice for non-D Rh disease in a modern UK-comparable setting; it cannot support or refute an efficacy claim for that use, and no such claim is made from it in this synthesis.

## (b) Why results might differ between studies

There is no meaningful "between-study heterogeneity" question to answer for Domain C in the way Domain A's era/blinding analysis answers it for Rh disease, because there is no cluster of comparative studies to be heterogeneous — each case report describes a single patient's course under a co-administered bundle of treatments (phototherapy plus IVIG plus, in most cases, exchange transfusion and/or simple transfusion), with no way to isolate IVIG's individual contribution. Several cases explicitly show IVIG being used alongside, not in place of, exchange transfusion (C2, C4, C10, C11), and one (C10, Wagner 2000, PMID 10827261) is a case where phototherapy, furosemide, albumin *and* IVIG together still did not prevent exchange transfusion — a null anecdote, given equal billing per CLAUDE.md rule 7, even though "equal billing" for n=1 case reports necessarily means much less than it does for the null RCTs in Domain A.

The one genuine mechanistic distinction in this domain — and the reason the brief singles out Kell disease specifically — is set out in `03-synthesis/00-mechanism.md`: Vaughan et al. (N Engl J Med 1998, PMID 9504940) showed in vitro that anti-Kell antibodies specifically and dose-dependently suppress the growth of Kell-positive erythroid progenitors from cord blood, and the companion clinical study (Vaughan et al., Am J Obstet Gynecol 1994, PMID 8030708) found anti-Kell-alloimmunised anaemic fetuses had reduced reticulocytosis and erythroblastosis compared with matched anti-D-alloimmunised fetuses at the same degree of anaemia. In other words, Kell-mediated anaemia is disproportionately a failure of red cell *production* (bone marrow suppression) rather than accelerated *destruction* of a normally produced red cell mass. Since IVIG's proposed mechanism (per the same mechanism note) is Fc-receptor blockade slowing macrophage-mediated clearance of antibody-coated red cells, this mechanism has, in principle, little to offer a disease process whose predominant driver is not peripheral destruction at all. **This is stated here as a plausibility argument grounded in the retrieved mechanistic literature, not as an efficacy finding — no comparative trial evidence exists in Kell disease (this domain) to confirm or refute that IVIG is in fact less effective, and this synthesis does not assert that it is.** Case C1 (Paez 2026, anti-k, PMID 41930090) is at least consistent with the plausibility argument: haemoglobin continued to fall despite IVIG, erythropoietin and iron, and correction was ultimately achieved only by red cell transfusion — but this is one uncontrolled case and cannot bear more evidential weight than that. A separate, IVIG-free case (Morales Painamil et al. 2023, Int J Neonatal Screen, PMID 37218889, cited in `00-mechanism.md`) describes an anti-Kell-alloimmunised infant with hyporegenerative anaemia after four intrauterine transfusions, managed with transfusion, oral iron and erythropoietin rather than IVIG at all — illustrative of the same production-failure mechanism, but not a Domain C extraction row since IVIG was not part of that infant's management.

## (c) Dose/timing used

Dose and timing data are sparse and, where given at all, are almost never the primary focus of the case report. Only one case in this domain reports a specific numeric IVIG dose: Sato et al. 1991 (C12, Infant 1, anti-E+anti-c, PMID 1709770) used 1 g/kg, given after hyperbilirubinaemia proved refractory to conventional phototherapy, with no adverse effects observed. No other Domain C case or the Healsmith cohort (C15) reports a dose, infusion duration, or number of repeat doses in enough detail to extract (all marked "not stated in abstract" in the extraction table). The one case with an explicit repeat-dosing course is Gustavsen et al. 2020 (C6, anti-Ku/Kell, PMID 31948214), in which "repeated infusions of intravenous immunoglobulin" were continued, together with iron supplementation, until the infant's haemoglobin normalised at three months of age — the exact number and interval of doses is not stated, but this is the single most direct anecdotal data point in the whole evidence base (Domains A–C) bearing on prolonged/repeat IVIG dosing, and is cross-referenced in Domain D. Timing, where stated at all, is uniformly postnatal-rescue (given after jaundice/anaemia/haemolysis was already identified, e.g. C2, C4, C7, C9, C10), never postnatal-prophylactic; no case in this domain describes prophylactic postnatal IVIG regardless of bilirubin trajectory (contrast the Leiden/Brazil prophylactic protocols in Domain A).

## (d) Where it failed or was null

Two anecdotes stand out as failures. C10 (Wagner 2000, anti-Ce, PMID 10827261) is the clearest: despite double phototherapy, furosemide, albumin infusion and IVIG together, the infant's total serum bilirubin continued to rise and exchange transfusion was ultimately required (with prompt resolution after exchange). C1 (Paez 2026, anti-k, PMID 41930090) shows a slower-motion failure specific to the Kell mechanism: haemoglobin continued to decline despite IVIG, erythropoietin and iron, and only red cell transfusion (using the mother's own k-negative blood, since compatible donor units were unavailable) reversed the anaemia. Set against these, several cases report apparently favourable courses with IVIG as part of the treatment bundle (C5, C7, C8, C9, C13) — but because none has a comparator arm, "apparently favourable" cannot be distinguished from the natural history of a case selected, and reported, because it resolved well. No systematic null-versus-positive pattern comparable to Domain A's era-effect can be drawn from case reports; the honest statement is that both null and apparently-positive anecdotes exist in roughly equal number, and neither can be weighted as evidence of effect in either direction.

## (e) Implication for the unit guideline

**GRADE: Very low certainty for any claim about IVIG's efficacy in Kell, anti-c, anti-E or mixed non-D alloimmune HDFN.** Reason for downgrade: study design alone (13 case reports, 1 two-patient case series, 1 uncontrolled descriptive cohort — no RCT, quasi-RCT or comparative cohort of any kind identified) plus serious imprecision (n=1 in the great majority of rows) and, for the Kell-specific question, indirectness (Domain A's Rh-disease trial evidence cannot be extrapolated to Kell disease, because the mechanism note above shows the two conditions plausibly have different underlying pathophysiology). **No direct evidence identified** that IVIG reduces exchange transfusion, phototherapy duration, or any other outcome in Kell, anti-c, anti-E or mixed-antibody HDFN — a guideline recommendation for or against IVIG use in these antibody types cannot rest on trial evidence, because none exists, and would have to rest instead on: (i) extrapolation from the Rh-disease trial base in Domain A, with the important caveat that this extrapolation is of doubtful validity specifically for Kell disease given its distinct erythroid-suppression mechanism; (ii) the case-report literature summarised here, understood explicitly as hypothesis-generating rather than effect-estimating; and (iii) grey-literature guidance (NICE, BSH, NHS England Ig Commissioning Criteria) if any such guidance specifically addresses non-D antibody HDFN, which is a question for the guidance-table exercise and is not answered here (guidance is not evidence, per CLAUDE.md rule 8). Practically, for Kell disease specifically, the mechanistic argument suggests IVIG is a poor biological fit for a bone-marrow-suppression-driven anaemia and that erythropoietin/iron/transfusion support (as used in C1 and in the IVIG-free Morales Painamil 2023 case) may be a more mechanistically coherent supportive strategy than IVIG — but this is this synthesis's own inference from mechanism, explicitly flagged as such, and is not a finding drawn from any comparative outcome data, because none exist.
