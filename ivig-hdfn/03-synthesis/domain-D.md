# Domain D — Dose, timing, repeat dosing and thresholds

This domain is cross-sectional over the Domain A (and, where noted, Domain C) trial/case base rather than an independently searched evidence set. Domain B (`03-synthesis/domain-B.md`) had not yet been written at the time this domain was compiled — **this is a gap**: any ABO-specific dose/timing pattern in Domain B's extraction rows is not reflected below and should be cross-checked against this file once Domain B exists. One targeted supplementary PubMed search was run (below) specifically for direct trial evidence on prophylactic-vs-rescue timing or repeat-dose strategy; it found none. All dose/timing/repeat-dose/comparator data below are read directly from `02-extraction/extraction.csv` (rows A1–A7) and `02-extraction/extraction-C.csv` (row C6), by their id, not re-described from the original trial reports.

Search: PubMed, 20 Sep 2026 (full string and screening detail in `01-search-log/searches-CD.md`, search D1).

## (a) What the evidence shows, and in whom

**No trial in the retrieved evidence base randomised or directly compared prophylactic-versus-rescue IVIG timing, or compared different repeat-dose strategies/intervals, as its primary research question.** The supplementary search for such a trial returned 9 records, of which 3 (PMID 26159803, 24514437, 21422084) are already extracted/synthesised in Domain A and 6 were off-topic on screening (adult autoimmune haemolytic anaemia, fetal/neonatal alloimmune thrombocytopenia, alloimmune neutropenia, and monoclonal-anti-D/Rh-antibody mechanism reviews — see the search log for the full exclusion list). What Domain D can answer, therefore, is only what can be read across the seven Rh trials Domain A already extracted (A1–A7), each of which used a fixed, single-arm-per-study dose/timing/repeat-dose regimen rather than randomising that variable, plus the one Domain C anecdote (C6) with an explicit repeat-dosing course.

**Dose (0.5 vs 1 g/kg):** the extraction table's `ivig_dose` column shows a spread from 500 mg/kg (A1 Rübo, A3 Dağoğlu, A7 Santos/Brazil) up to 0.75 g/kg (A6 Smits-Wintjens/Leiden) and 1 g/kg equivalents (A2 Voto's 800 mg/kg/day ×3 days is a cumulative 2.4 g/kg over the course), with A5 (Nasseri) using 0.5 g/kg every 12h ×3 doses (cumulative 1.5 g/kg). The only trial that directly randomised dose as its comparison is A4 (Elalfy 2010, PMID 20924607, 3-arm trial: 0.5 g/kg vs 1 g/kg vs conventional phototherapy alone), and per `domain-A.md` (c), it found the 1 g/kg arm numerically more effective at avoiding exchange transfusion than the 0.5 g/kg arm (0% vs 5% exchange transfusion, against 22% in the conventional-phototherapy comparator arm; p=0.03 across groups per the extraction table), while both doses shortened phototherapy duration and admission similarly. This is the only head-to-head dose-comparison RCT identified anywhere in the evidence base (Domains A–D); no equivalent dose-comparison trial exists for ABO disease (Domain B, not yet available) or for the Domain C antibody types (case reports report a single dose per patient, most commonly 1 g/kg where stated at all — see C12, PMID 1709770 — with no within-study comparison).

**Infusion duration:** reported in only two of the seven Domain A rows: A1 (Rübo) used a 2-hour infusion, and A5 (Nasseri) used 4 hours per dose (×3 doses). The remaining five rows (A2–A4, A6, A7) mark infusion duration "not stated in abstract." No Domain C case reports an infusion duration. There is therefore no basis in the retrieved evidence to recommend one infusion duration over another; any guideline duration would have to rest on product SPC guidance (a grey-literature source, not evidence — see (e) below) rather than on trial data.

**Prophylactic vs rescue timing:** this is answerable only descriptively, by reading each trial's own protocol timing off the extraction table's `timing` column, not from any trial that randomised timing itself. Domain A's `domain-A.md` (c) already makes this point explicitly: "No trial in this domain directly randomised prophylactic-vs-rescue timing as its comparison." Reading the table: A6 (Smits-Wintjens/Leiden) and A7 (Santos/Brazil) — the two modern, double-blind, placebo-controlled, null trials — both used **prophylactic** dosing (A6: "given regardless of bilirubin trajectory, stratified by IUT status"; A7: "within the first 6 hours of life," irrespective of bilirubin trajectory). A5 (Nasseri) used explicit **rescue** dosing, triggered by a defined bilirubin-rise rate (≥0.5 mg/dL/h). A1, A3, A4 describe "early" or "early/rescue" postnatal timing that is not cleanly classifiable as either pure prophylaxis or pure rescue from the abstract text alone (marked accordingly in the table). Because the two trials using prophylactic timing are also the two null, lowest-risk-of-bias trials, and because timing co-varies with era and blinding in Domain A's dataset (see `domain-A.md` (b)), it is not possible to attribute the null result specifically to prophylactic timing as opposed to modern phototherapy intensity or blinding/placebo control — all three factors move together across the same two trials. **No direct evidence identified** that isolates timing (prophylactic vs rescue) as an independent variable.

## (b) Why results might differ between studies

The apparent "dose-response" signal in A4 (Elalfy) sits inside a single 3-arm trial with unclear blinding/allocation concealment (per its own extraction row) and a comparator arm not described as reflecting intensive multi-light phototherapy — the indirectness flag already recorded in A4's `key_limitation` field. It cannot be reconciled against, or triangulated with, any other dose-comparison trial, because none exists; a single trial's point estimate, however clean-looking, is not the same as a replicated dose-response relationship. Similarly, the repeat-dose regimens (A2's 3-daily-dose, A5's 3-dose-every-12h) and the single-dose regimens (A1, A3, A4, A6, A7) were not compared head-to-head anywhere; any apparent difference in effect between single-dose and repeat-dose trials is confounded by era, blinding, antibody type (A5 is a mixed Rh+ABO trial) and phototherapy intensity in exactly the pattern Domain A's (b) describes for the efficacy question generally, and the same caution applies here.

## (c) Dose/timing used

Already answered directly, by extraction-table row, in (a) above; not repeated here per the instruction to cross-reference rather than re-describe.

## (d) Where it failed or was null

The two trials using prophylactic timing (A6, A7) are the same two trials that were null for exchange transfusion in Domain A — but as noted in (a), this cannot be disentangled from those trials' era and blinding advantages. No trial using rescue timing (A5) or the less clearly classified "early" timing (A1, A3, A4) was null; all four were positive. Read uncritically, this could look like "prophylactic timing is associated with null results and rescue/early timing with positive results" — but this is exactly the same confound as Domain A's era-effect (the null trials are also the modern, blinded, intensive-phototherapy trials), and asserting a timing-specific causal claim from this pattern would be exactly the kind of indirect inference CLAUDE.md rule 5 requires flagging rather than asserting. The honest statement is that timing, era, and trial quality are perfectly confounded in this dataset, and no trial exists that varies timing while holding the others constant.

## (e) Implication for the unit guideline

**GRADE for the dose-comparison question (0.5 vs 1 g/kg): Low certainty that 1 g/kg is more effective than 0.5 g/kg at avoiding exchange transfusion in term Rh-D disease**, based on a single 3-arm RCT (A4, Elalfy 2010) with unclear blinding/allocation concealment, an indirectness concern (comparator phototherapy arm not confirmed as intensive/modern-standard), and no replication. Downgraded for risk of bias and imprecision (single trial, single centre, n=90 across 3 arms) — one level rather than two because the direction of effect (higher dose → lower exchange-transfusion rate) is at least biologically coherent with a dose-dependent Fc-receptor-blockade mechanism, to the extent that mechanism is accepted (see `00-mechanism.md`).

**GRADE for infusion duration: No direct evidence identified.** Only 2/7 Domain A trials report a duration at all (2h and 4h-per-dose), with no comparison between durations and no data at all outside Domain A. Any guideline infusion duration would have to rest on product SPC guidance for the specific IVIG product the unit stocks (grey literature, not trial evidence — to be captured in the guidance table, not asserted here) and/or on general neonatal infusion-tolerability practice, not on the trial base identified in this review.

**GRADE for prophylactic vs rescue timing: No direct evidence identified.** No trial (in Domains A, B, C or D's supplementary search) randomised timing as an independent variable; the apparent association between prophylactic timing and null results in Domain A's two best trials is fully confounded with era and blinding and must not be read as a timing effect. A guideline recommendation on timing would have to rest on: (i) the practical observation that the two trials most directly applicable to a modern UK NICU (A6, A7 — both explicitly using intensive/modern-era phototherapy) used prophylactic protocols and were null, which argues against a policy of *routine* prophylactic IVIG under modern phototherapy specifically because it was tested this way and failed to show benefit — this is evidence against routine prophylactic use under modern co-intervention, not evidence for or against rescue-timed use, which was tested in a different (older, non-blinded) trial context (A5); and (ii) extrapolation from the general principle, untested here, that a rescue trigger tied to a defined bilirubin-rise rate (as in A5: ≥0.5 mg/dL/h) at least targets treatment to infants demonstrating ongoing haemolysis, which is a plausibility argument, not a finding.

**GRADE for repeat-dose strategy and optimal interval: No direct evidence identified.** No trial compared repeat-dosing intervals or number of doses. The only data points bearing on repeat dosing at all are anecdotal: A5 (Nasseri, 0.5 g/kg every 12h ×3 doses, in a positive but very-low-certainty trial with a late-anaemia signal — 11.8% vs 0%, p=0.48/NS) and Domain C's C6 (Gustavsen 2020, anti-Ku, PMID 31948214 — "repeated infusions" continued until haemoglobin normalised at 3 months, no interval specified). A repeat-dosing recommendation would have to rest on extrapolation from these two single non-comparative data points, from single-dose trial regimens generalised upward, and from the product SPC's licensed dosing schedule — not from any comparative repeat-dose trial, because none was found. Given A5's late-anaemia signal (albeit non-significant and abstract-only), a guideline permitting repeat dosing should flag late anaemia as a specific outcome to monitor for, per CLAUDE.md rule 7's requirement that null/harm signals get equal billing.
