## (a) Where the guidance documents agree

Every current guidance document identified — NICE CG98, the AAP 2022 guideline, the current (2025/2026) CPS revision, the BSH 2016 transfusion guideline, and the representative Australian/NZ documents — agrees on the same structural position: **IVIG is a second-line adjunct, not a first-line treatment, reserved for infants with DAT/Coombs-positive isoimmune (Rh or ABO) haemolytic disease whose bilirubin is rising toward, or has reached, a level close to the exchange-transfusion threshold despite intensive phototherapy.** None recommends prophylactic or routine IVIG for all Rh/ABO disease irrespective of bilirubin trajectory. The dose that recurs across almost every document is 0.5–1 g/kg (NICE and the BSH-linked UK network material specify 500 mg/kg over 4 hours; AAP 2022 and the current CPS both specify 0.5–1 g/kg over 2 hours with an option to repeat once at 12 hours) — this dose range is essentially unchanged from the 1990s-2000s trial era despite everything that has happened to the surrounding evidence base in the interim (Domain A).

There is also broad agreement, where documents discuss it explicitly, that **evidence quality is low**. The AAP 2022 Technical Report says so in its own PubMed-indexed abstract (PMID 35927519): "IVIG has unclear benefit for preventing exchange transfusion in infants with isoimmune hemolytic disease, with a possible risk of harm due to necrotizing enterocolitis... Limited evidence for effectiveness with some evidence of risk of harm support the revised recommendations to limit IVIG use." The international expert-panel guideline published in the British Journal of Haematology in March 2022 (DOI 10.1111/bjh.18170; its content is independently corroborated via two PubMed-verified secondary sources — the response Letter by Hansen, Akkök & Watchko, PMID 35993219, BJH 2022;199(3):452-453, and a Chinese-language interpretation, PMID 36398541, Zhongguo Dang Dai Er Ke Za Zhi 2022;24(11):1183-1188) goes further and states outright that **IVIG should not be used routinely to reduce exchange transfusion in Rh- or ABO-mediated HDN**, describing its own recommendations as "formulated with rigorous methods, but with the lower quality of evidence" (per the Chinese interpretation's English abstract). This is essentially the same conclusion Domain A reached independently from the primary trial literature: the historical large-effect trials do not survive risk-of-bias stratification, and the two best-conducted RCTs (Leiden, PMID 21422084; Brazil, PMID 22882285) are null.

## (b) Where the documents diverge, and why it matters

Despite the shared structural position, there is a real divergence in **how far each document has updated its framing to reflect that the underlying trial base has flipped from positive to null**:

- **AAP 2022** is the most explicit and most recently and thoroughly revised. Its Technical Report is a systematic evidence review through March 2022 (i.e. it necessarily captured the Leiden and Brazilian null RCTs and the Louis 2014 / Zwiers 2018 risk-of-bias-stratified reviews that Domain A relies on) and the guideline text visibly downgrades IVIG accordingly: "may be provided," conditional on an "escalation of care threshold" rather than a blanket trigger, explicitly citing "possible risk of harm due to necrotizing enterocolitis" as a countervailing consideration. This is a guideline that has read the null-trial literature and revised its recommendation downward in direct response.

- **The international expert-panel guideline (BJH 2022)** goes further still, concluding IVIG should not be used routinely at all — but even this was immediately contested in the peer-reviewed literature by its own commentators (Hansen, Akkök & Watchko, PMID 35993219), who argued the "no routine use" framing is fine for prophylactic dosing but too broad when applied to the genuinely emergent scenario of rapidly rising bilirubin with exchange transfusion imminent or unavailable — i.e. even the most evidence-current international guidance body has an active, published dispute about exactly the population (severe, near-exchange-threshold disease) where IVIG might still plausibly help, and that dispute is unresolved in the peer-reviewed record as of this search.

- **NICE CG98** is the outlier that most needs flagging for the unit guideline. Its core recommendation text (500 mg/kg over 4 hours, triggered by SBR rising >8.5 µmol/L/hour) reads as essentially unrevised since the 2010 original guideline. The October 2023 exceptional surveillance review touched TSB-threshold wording for assay variability and prolonged-jaundice urine-culture advice — **it did not, on the evidence available this session, revisit the IVIG recommendation or its evidence base** in light of either the AAP's 2022 re-evaluation or the international expert panel's 2022 "no routine use" conclusion, both published in the same year as NICE's own surveillance review. This could not be fully confirmed because the underlying NICE evidence pages were unreachable this session (see could-not-verify-F.md), but nothing in the WebSearch-available material suggests the CG98 IVIG recommendation itself was substantively re-examined in 2023. If this holds, **NICE CG98 is the guidance document in this table most likely to still be resting on the pre-2011, risk-of-bias-unstratified trial base** (the Alcock & Liley 2002 Cochrane review and its constituent 1990s open-label trials) that Domain A found does not survive stratification — this is the single most important guidance-vs-evidence gap this domain identified and should be flagged prominently in the unit guideline draft and any *Infant* manuscript, with the caveat that NICE's actual GDG evidence-statement wording could not be independently retrieved this session to confirm it verbatim.

- **CPS shows the clearest before/after case of a guidance body having actually revised its position.** The 2007 CPS statement recommended IVIG "Grade A" citing a pooled RR of 0.28 (95% CI 0.17–0.47, NNT 3) — this is (or is drawn from the same evidence stream as) the Alcock & Liley 2002 Cochrane estimate, the risk-of-bias-unstratified figure. The current 2025/2026 CPS revision (PMID 42488358) instead uses AAP-style conditional, threshold-based language ("should be considered... if an exchange transfusion cannot be readily performed," triggered by a "pre-exchange transfusion threshold"), which structurally mirrors the AAP 2022 downgrade rather than repeating the 2007 Grade-A framing. This is a second guidance body that appears to have followed the evidence away from the early positive trials — though this domain could not confirm from the material accessible this session whether the current CPS text explicitly discusses the null RCTs or explains the change in reasoning (flagged in could-not-verify-F.md); the shift in wording is nonetheless consistent with the same evidence-driven downgrade seen in AAP.

- **BSH (UK)**, the document most directly relevant to an Imperial NICU guideline given lead-author affiliation with Imperial College Healthcare NHS Trust, could not be fully text-verified this session, but the WebSearch-available framing ("IVIG use should be considered... if there is delay in commencing an exchange transfusion due to availability of blood") is narrower and more circumstance-specific than NICE's rate-of-rise trigger, closer in spirit to the AAP/CPS "when exchange transfusion is not readily available" framing than to NICE's more permissive "as an adjunct to intensive phototherapy" wording. Whether BSH's full reference list still leans on the 1990s trial base could not be established this session (full text unreachable) — this is a specific, material gap for the unit guideline team to close by obtaining the primary BJH 2016 article and its 2020 addendum directly.

- **Dutch guidance** is of particular interest because the Leiden group itself (Lopriore/Smits-Wintjens) authored the null Rh RCT (PMID 21422084) that is central to Domain A's downgrade. This domain could not determine, given the WebFetch block, whether the Dutch NVK national richtlijn has been revised to reflect its own country's trial finding, or still carries an older recommendation — this is flagged as a priority item to resolve before finalising the *Infant* manuscript's international comparison, since "does the trial's own country's national guideline still recommend the intervention the trial refuted" is exactly the kind of pointed comparison a peer reviewer would ask for.

- **NHS England commissioning governance** sits one level above clinical guidance and was not found to make an independent efficacy claim; its function is to gate NHS funding and to standardise outcome recording via the National Immunoglobulin Database, not to set clinical thresholds. The 2025 edition's removal of the historical Red/Blue/Grey evidence-strength colour-coding is notable in itself — if HDFN's specific classification carried a "Grey" (limited/little-no evidence) label in an earlier edition, that label's disappearance in a schema redesign should not be read as an implicit evidence upgrade; this domain could not confirm the pre-2025 or current classification for HDFN specifically and flags it as an open item, since it is directly relevant to whether a unit choosing to prescribe IVIG for HDFN needs Immunoglobulin Assessment Panel sign-off.

- **Scandinavian and Australian/NZ guidance**, so far as could be established (weakly, in the Norwegian case — see caveats in the guidance table and could-not-verify-F.md), sit closer to the historical trial-era framing (dose-only, rate-of-rise or proximity-to-threshold triggers) without visible reference to the 2022 re-evaluations. A real-world Norwegian audit finding that only 4% of HDN infants nationally underwent exchange transfusion and under 20% received IVIG in a national one-year cohort suggests that, whatever the written guideline says, actual practice in at least one Scandinavian system has already drifted toward much more conservative IVIG use than the guideline's wording alone would suggest — a guidance-vs-practice gap layered on top of the guidance-vs-evidence gap.

## (c) UK product-licensing status — a governance point distinct from clinical guidance

None of the UK-licensed IVIG products checked (Privigen; Kiovig, found for comparison) carries haemolytic disease of the newborn as a licensed indication on its SPC. UK use of IVIG for HDFN is therefore **off-label/unlicensed use of an on-market, licensed product**, authorised through unit/network clinical guidance (NICE/BSH/local neonatal network) rather than through the product licence itself. This has two practical implications for the unit guideline: (i) standard off-label-use governance (documented clinical justification, usually consultant sign-off) applies in addition to whatever the NICE/BSH threshold specifies; (ii) the SPC's own safety text is nonetheless directly relevant to safety monitoring, since IVIG preparations can themselves contain anti-A/anti-B isoagglutinin antibodies capable of causing iatrogenic haemolysis in a baby already being treated for haemolysis — manufacturers address this with an isoagglutinin-reduction manufacturing step (immunoaffinity chromatography, in Privigen's case), but this detail could not be independently confirmed against the primary emc page this session (WebFetch blocked) and should be re-verified from the primary SPC before it is relied on in the unit guideline's safety section.

## (d) Implication for the unit guideline and the *Infant* manuscript

**The central guidance-vs-evidence gap in Domain F is a two-speed picture: some guidance bodies (AAP 2022, the 2022 international expert panel, and — on the current wording — CPS) have visibly revised their IVIG recommendation downward in step with the null trials Domain A identified, explicitly naming both the uncertain benefit and the NEC signal; others (most importantly NICE CG98, the UK document an Imperial NICU guideline would otherwise default to) show no confirmed evidence, from what was accessible this session, of having revisited the IVIG recommendation's evidence base since 2010, despite two major international re-evaluations happening in the same calendar year (2022) as NICE's own most recent surveillance activity (2023).** A unit guideline built by simply adopting NICE CG98's IVIG wording verbatim would be adopting a recommendation that, on the balance of what Domain A found and what AAP/the international panel now say, rests on evidence that does not hold up under risk-of-bias stratification — this should be stated explicitly in the unit guideline's rationale section and in any *Infant* manuscript, with the specific recommendation that the guideline team obtain and read the NICE CG98 full evidence review directly (this domain could not do so, given the network restriction) before finalising unit wording, since it is possible NICE's underlying evidence tables already reflect a more nuanced position than the surface recommendation text this session could access.
