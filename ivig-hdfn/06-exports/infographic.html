Key take-aways for the unit guideline group

  1. IVIG's historical evidence base does not survive contemporary trial conditions. The two best-conducted Rh trials, under modern intensive phototherapy, found no benefit at all.
  2. ABO disease has a weaker case than Rh disease, not a smaller version of the same case — no low-risk-of-bias ABO trial has ever been conducted.
  3. The necrotising enterocolitis signal is real enough to monitor for, but not proven causal — the best-controlled study found no association once confounding by indication was addressed.
  4. IVIG must never delay a clinically indicated exchange transfusion — this is the one point of universal agreement across every guidance document and the trial evidence.
  5. DAT-negative ABO disease and non-Rh/ABO antibodies (Kell, anti-c, anti-E) are genuine evidence vacuums, not extrapolations from the main trial base — treat them as distinct unknowns requiring individual clinical judgement.
  6. Late anaemia surveillance should be applied to all HDN infants with significant haemolysis or IUT history, regardless of IVIG exposure — it is a feature of the disease, not the treatment.
  7. NICE CG98's evidence base should be directly re-verified before the unit guideline defaults to its wording — international bodies revised their recommendations in 2022 in ways NICE does not appear to have matched.
Two items must be resolved before the unit guideline is ratified: (1) direct verification of NICE CG98's underlying evidence citations, and (2) direct verification of current UK product SPCs (dosing, infusion rate, isoagglutinin content) for whichever IVIG brand is actually stocked.
