# Antenatal Dexamethasone Dosing Interval — Unit Guideline (Draft)

*Draft for departmental review. Every recommendation carries a certainty label and a reference number pointing to `acs-interval-synthesis.md`. Guideline-wording claims attributed to RCOG/NICE/ACOG in this draft are reconstructed from secondary sources (network access to primary guideline sites was blocked throughout this review) and must be independently verified against primary text before adoption.*

---

## 1. The finding this guideline exists to act on

The unit's practice of giving **dexamethasone 12 mg IM at a 12-hourly interval** (2 doses, 24 mg total) is not recommended by RCOG Green-top Guideline No. 74, the WHO 2022 recommendation, ACOG guidance, or a 2023 European perinatal consensus guideline — none of which name this specific combination of dose and interval. It is not the subject of any randomised trial: the dedicated Cochrane review of antenatal corticosteroid regimens (Williams et al 2022) identifies only three regimen-comparison studies in the entire global literature, none of which is a dexamethasone-interval trial. *[Certainty: High — synthesis §Q1, §Q3]*

This regimen does, however, have real and longstanding clinical precedent elsewhere: it has been described in UK practice (Hope Hospital, Salford, 2003) and used as the background regimen in a 2021 Malaysian RCT, and modelled in a 2024 animal study as reflecting Singapore practice. **In more than two decades of apparent use in multiple health systems, it has never been the subject of a trial measuring its efficacy or safety against the standard regimens.** *[Certainty: High as a factual finding — synthesis §Q1, §Q3]*

## 2. Recommended regimen and rationale

Use one of the two guideline-endorsed, trial-tested regimens:

| Regimen | Dose | Interval | Doses | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WHO/ACOG standard | 6 mg IM | 12-hourly | 4 | 24 mg |
| RCOG/ASTEROID | 12 mg IM | 24-hourly | 2 | 24 mg |

Either is acceptable. The ASTEROID trial (n=1346/1509) found no meaningful difference between the RCOG dexamethasone regimen and standard betamethasone in the 2-year composite death-or-neurosensory-disability outcome (adjRR 0.97, 95% CI 0.83–1.13). *[Certainty: Moderate — synthesis §Q6]* **Do not give 12 mg at a 12-hourly interval.** This is neither regimen; it has no trial evidence of any kind behind it. *[Certainty: High that the evidence gap exists — synthesis §Q1]*

## 3. What to do when delivery is imminent

**Give the first dose immediately, regardless of how imminent delivery appears.** A partial course reduces risk relative to no course at all; this is well established in the wider ACS literature. *[Certainty: Moderate]*

**Do not compress the interval or bring forward a second dose solely to "complete the course" before an anticipated delivery.** The mechanistic evidence assembled for this review is explicit: the fastest biochemical response to glucocorticoid exposure documented in any experimental system (human/animal in-vitro lung tissue) takes approximately 12 hours of *continuous* exposure to become first detectable, and meaningful surfactant protein induction requires 24–48 hours. A second dose landing roughly an hour before birth contributes an increment of exposure shorter than the fastest effect ever recorded in any system studied. There is no plausible mechanism by which it can help the baby's lungs in that time. *[Certainty: mechanistic inference, not a direct trial finding, but very well-grounded — synthesis §Q4]*

**This is not a reason to withhold the second dose if it falls due for other reasons** (i.e., if delivery is not in fact imminent and the standard interval simply happens to fall at 12 or 24 hours) — this recommendation applies specifically to the practice of *deliberately bringing a dose forward* because delivery is thought to be imminent within the hour.

The harm side of this calculation does not favour compression either: several cohorts show *increased*, not decreased, neonatal hypoglycaemia risk at short ACS-to-delivery intervals, and human data show even brief or remote corticosteroid exposure is associated with lasting HPA-axis suppression effects. Giving an additional dose in the final hour before birth cannot be assumed harm-free simply because it has not had time to help. *[Certainty: absence of evidence for benefit; genuinely unknown, not evidence-based-safe, for harm — synthesis §Q4]*

## 4. Glycaemic monitoring protocol

Antenatal corticosteroids cause clinically significant, transient maternal hyperglycaemia in most women, diabetic and non-diabetic, regardless of agent or interval, typically peaking within 9–24 hours and resolving within 72 hours. *[Certainty: Moderate — synthesis §Q5]*

- For all women with diabetes (gestational or pre-existing) receiving ACS: increase subcutaneous insulin by 50–80%, or commence variable-rate IV insulin infusion, with capillary glucose monitoring at least hourly during administration and for 72 hours afterward. *(Reconstructed from Joint British Diabetes Societies guidance via secondary sources — verify against current primary JBDS text before finalising this protocol.)*
- Data specific to this unit's actual 12 mg/12-hourly regimen (from institutions that have incidentally used it) show a real but self-limited, day-1-predominant hyperglycaemic pattern — not obviously more alarming in shape than the standard 24-hourly literature, though no direct interval comparison exists to confirm equivalence. *[Certainty: Low-Moderate — synthesis §Q5]*
- Where maternal hyperglycaemia occurs in the 24 hours before birth, anticipate an increased risk of neonatal hypoglycaemia and ensure standard neonatal glucose monitoring is in place.

## 5. Documenting and communicating an incomplete course

Document explicitly: agent(s) given, exact doses and times, interval between doses, and gestational age at each dose. Where a course is left incomplete because delivery occurred before the second dose, or because the second dose was deliberately withheld per Section 3 above, record the reason.

**What "complete course" means in this unit, going forward:** a complete course is two doses of the RCOG regimen 24 hours apart, or four doses of the WHO/ACOG regimen 12 hours apart. A course using the 12 mg/12-hourly interval, however completed, should be documented as **non-standard** rather than as a routine complete course, given the total absence of evidence behind this specific combination — this is a data-quality and future-audit measure, not a implication that the baby did not benefit from the glucocorticoid exposure received.

## 6. Mixed-agent courses

Where a woman has received one dose of betamethasone and a second of dexamethasone (or vice versa), document as a mixed/non-standard course, both agents/doses/timing, and manage clinically with the same consideration as an incomplete course. On pharmacological grounds, both UK-available agents are plain phosphate esters (not depot preparations), and a mixed course delivers real combined glucocorticoid exposure — but does not replicate the tested profile of either single-agent regimen. *[Certainty: inference only, not graded clinical evidence — synthesis §Q7 scenario 6]*

## 7. Chorioamnionitis

ACS should still be given if delivery is not imminent; do not delay delivery to complete a course when infection is driving the clinical picture. This review did not generate dedicated interval-specific evidence for the chorioamnionitis scenario — this recommendation draws on the general chorioamnionitis-ACS literature, not on findings specific to this brief. *[Certainty: Low, extrapolated — synthesis §Q7 scenario 5]*

## 8. Audit measures

- Proportion of women receiving the 12 mg/12-hourly regimen before and after guideline adoption, as the primary implementation metric.
- Proportion of courses documented as complete (per Section 5's definition) vs incomplete vs non-standard/mixed.
- Rate of second doses given within 2 hours of delivery, cross-referenced against Section 3's recommendation.
- Maternal glycaemic control metrics (peak glucose, insulin requirement) by regimen used, to build the unit's own comparative dataset given the absence of external trial evidence.
- Neonatal hypoglycaemia rate by ACS-to-delivery interval, to locally monitor against the pattern described in synthesis §Q4–Q5.
