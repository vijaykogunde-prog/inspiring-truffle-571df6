## The three regimens — do not mix them up

| | Dose | Interval | Doses | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ✅ WHO / ACOG standard | 6 mg IM | **12-hourly** | 4 | 24 mg |
| ✅ RCOG / ASTEROID | 12 mg IM | **24-hourly** | 2 | 24 mg |
| ❌ **NOT RECOMMENDED** | 12 mg IM | **12-hourly** | 2 | 24 mg |

**The bottom row is not a licensed indication, not named in any guideline checked (RCOG/WHO/ACOG/European consensus), and has never been tested in a trial of any kind — for RDS, IVH, NEC, mortality, or neurodevelopment.** It combines the per-dose amount of the RCOG regimen with the interval of the WHO regimen. This combination has real precedent elsewhere (UK, Malaysia, Singapore, over 20+ years) — but precedent is not evidence.

## Decision tree: delivery is imminent

```
First dose due / already given?
│
├── First dose not yet given → GIVE IT NOW, regardless of how imminent
│   delivery appears. A partial course still helps. Do not withhold.
│
└── First dose given, second dose due, delivery expected very soon
    │
    ├── Second dose falls due at its SCHEDULED interval (12h if using
    │   WHO regimen, 24h if using RCOG regimen) → give it as scheduled.
    │
    └── Considering bringing the second dose FORWARD (e.g. to 12h on
        a 24h regimen) specifically to "complete the course" before
        anticipated delivery
        → DO NOT. No plausible mechanism for benefit if delivery
          follows within ~1 hour (fastest documented biological
          response to glucocorticoid exposure takes ~12h continuous
          exposure to even begin). Harm risk (neonatal hypoglycaemia,
          HPA-axis loading) is NOT reduced by giving the dose late —
          if anything, short-interval cohorts show MORE hypoglycaemia,
          not less.
```

## The one-line summary for the "why" question

*"There is no evidence this practice works better, and no evidence it's as safe as the standard regimens — it has simply never been tested. Use one of the two regimens that has."*

## Maternal glucose monitoring (any regimen)

- Expect significant hyperglycaemia, diabetic or not — typically peaks 9–24h post-dose, resolves by ~72h.
- Diabetic/GDM women: ↑ insulin 50–80% or start VRIII; check capillary glucose ≥ hourly during administration.
- Maternal hyperglycaemia in the 24h before birth → anticipate neonatal hypoglycaemia; ensure standard neonatal glucose monitoring.

## Documentation

Record for every course: agent(s), exact dose, exact times, interval achieved, GA at each dose. Label a 12mg/12-hourly course, or any mixed-agent course, as **non-standard** — not a failure, just accurate data for this baby's team and for unit audit.

## What this is based on

No RCT of dexamethasone 12mg-12h-vs-24h exists (confirmed via the dedicated 2022 Cochrane regimen review). The only interval RCT for *either* drug used betamethasone (Khandelwal 2012, n=228): equivalent RDS, but higher NEC with the 12-hourly interval (6.2% vs 0%, p=.03) — small, unreplicated in 14 years, and not directly transferable to dexamethasone. Full reasoning in the synthesis.
