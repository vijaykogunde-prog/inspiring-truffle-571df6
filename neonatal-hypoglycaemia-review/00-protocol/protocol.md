# Protocol — Management of Hypoglycaemia in Term and Preterm Infants

## Scope statement

**Track:** Narrative evidence synthesis, governance/teaching register — not a systematic review, not (by default) a manuscript. No PROSPERO registration. Search process is nonetheless documented in full (`01-search-log/searches.md`) to the standard expected of a modern narrative review.

**Primary question:** How should hypoglycaemia be defined, detected, and managed in term and preterm infants in the first days of life and beyond, and what evidence underpins current UK (BAPM) practice?

**Secondary questions:** as enumerated in RESEARCH-BRIEF.md §3 (10 questions spanning definitions, thresholds by gestation, point-of-care vs laboratory diagnosis, the BAPM 48h pathway, preterm-specific evidence, term neurodevelopmental outcome, MRI in hypoglycaemic seizure, hyperglycaemia follow-up, and the UK-guidance-vs-trial-evidence tension).

**Population:** term, late preterm, moderate preterm, very preterm, and extremely preterm infants, birth through early childhood follow-up. Explicitly includes both hypoglycaemia and hyperglycaemia.

**Excluded from primary scope (signposted only):** hypoglycaemia persisting or recurring beyond the first 48–72h with a differential diagnosis of congenital hyperinsulinism, inborn errors of metabolism, or endocrinopathy — this has its own specialist pathway (critical sample, endocrine referral) and is not re-derived here.

**Target audience:** consultant neonatologist, UK Level 3 NICU. Register is consultant-level throughout — no primer content.

## Four framing points established before searching (RESEARCH-BRIEF.md §4)

1. **Screening threshold ≠ injury threshold.** Operational thresholds that trigger clinical action (analogous to phototherapy thresholds in jaundice) are set deliberately above the level at which neuroglycopenic injury is believed to occur. This synthesis keeps "the level at which we act" and "the level at which harm occurs" conceptually and typographically separate throughout — conflating them is flagged wherever it appears in the primary literature as a source of clinical/educational error.

2. **Units reported in both systems throughout.** UK practice: mmol/L. Much of the primary trial and older literature: mg/dL. Conversion factor used throughout: **1 mmol/L = 18.0 mg/dL** (so 2.6 mmol/L = 47 mg/dL; 2.0 mmol/L = 36 mg/dL). Every threshold value in this synthesis carries both units; where a source reports only one, the conversion is shown explicitly, not silently assumed.

3. **"Hypoglycaemia" is a proxy for neuroglycopenia, not a direct measurement of it.** There is no bedside test for brain glucose deprivation; blood/plasma glucose is used as a surrogate. This is why screening for neonatal hypoglycaemia sits awkwardly against classical screening-test principles (Wilson–Jungner-type criteria) — a point made explicitly in Alsweiler et al. 2022 (to be re-verified this session) and carried through Domain B/C.

4. **Three clinically distinct time-windows:**
   - (a) transitional physiology, birth to ~4h
   - (b) first 48–72h — the window targeted by BAPM's Framework and by the dominant RCT evidence (CHYLD, Sugar Babies, hPOD)
   - (c) beyond 72h / persistent or recurrent hypoglycaemia — a different differential diagnosis and pathway, out of primary scope (see exclusion above), signposted but not developed in depth.

## Directness — the central methodological issue in this topic

The dominant RCT evidence base (CHYLD, Sugar Babies, hPOD/pre-hPOD; all Liggins Institute/Auckland group, New Zealand) enrolled **late preterm (≥32 weeks in some protocols, predominantly ≥35 weeks) and term infants at risk of hypoglycaemia** — not the very preterm/extremely preterm population that dominates a UK Level 3 NICU caseload. Every claim in this synthesis states which gestational population it is drawn from, and every extrapolation from this trial base to very/extremely preterm infants is flagged explicitly at the point it is made, not left implicit. This directness problem is tracked as its own item in the cross-cutting synthesis (§6.5 of the brief — the preterm evidence-gap statement) rather than being diffused across domains.
