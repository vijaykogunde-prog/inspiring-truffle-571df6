## Access limitation, not a citation failure — flagged for transparency, now resolved

**bapm.org itself could not be directly retrieved by any domain this session.** Every `WebFetch` attempt to `www.bapm.org` (and, for Domain A specifically, also `pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov` and `www.mdpi.com` when accessed by direct URL rather than via the PubMed MCP connector) returned `EGRESS_BLOCKED`, including a repeat attempt from the parent session while assembling the final PDF. Every BAPM-specific numeric threshold reported in Domains A, B, and C's original text was therefore transcribed from peer-reviewed secondary characterisations (principally Giouleka et al. 2023, PMID 37508719, and Rusu et al. 2026, PMID 42194863) or from web-search-indexed page titles/snippets and NHS trust guidelines citing BAPM verbatim, not from bapm.org's primary text.

**Resolution: the user subsequently supplied the primary BAPM Framework document directly** (*Identification and Management of Neonatal Hypoglycaemia in the Full-Term Infant, Birth–72 hours*, January 2024, amended 20/06/2024 and 16/09/2024), which was read in full this session. This identified material corrections to the secondary-sourced figures — most importantly that BAPM's own operational threshold is a tiered 1.0/2.0/2.5 mmol/L system, not 2.6 mmol/L, and that a directly relevant threshold-comparison RCT (HypoEXIT, van Kempen et al. 2020, PMID 32023373) exists and was cited by BAPM itself but had not been found by any of the six domain search agents. Full correction record: `03-synthesis/bapm-primary-source-verification.md`. This item is retained here (rather than deleted) as a record of the search-process limitation that occurred and how it was ultimately closed — by a direct primary-source upload, not by expanded network access.

## Non-existent trial name, corrected rather than a failed citation

The research brief named a "COIN trial" for preterm insulin therapy (Domain G scope). No trial by that name exists in the peer-reviewed literature — Domain G ran a dedicated negative search confirming this. The trial the brief was almost certainly describing is the correctly-named **NIRTURE trial** (Beardsall et al. 2008, *NEJM*, PMID 18971490, DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa0803725), which is fully verified and used throughout Domain G's synthesis under its correct name. This is recorded here as a transparent correction to the brief itself, not as an unverifiable citation quietly dropped.
