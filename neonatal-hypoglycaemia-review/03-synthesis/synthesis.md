# Management of Hypoglycaemia (and Hyperglycaemia) in Term and Preterm Infants — Evidence Synthesis

**Prepared for:** consultant neonatologist, UK Level 3 NICU (Imperial College Healthcare NHS Trust). **Track:** narrative evidence synthesis, governance/teaching register — not a systematic review, not a manuscript. **Status:** all seven domains (A–G) complete; every claim below carries a PMID/DOI verified this session or is explicitly labelled as grey literature.

## Part 1 — Domain summaries

### Domain A — Definitions and operational thresholds (`domain-A-definitions.md`)
Cornblath et al.'s 2000 "operational threshold" framing (PMID 10790476) is the direct conceptual ancestor of BAPM's Framework. BAPM's document — retitled "...(Birth – 72 hours)" in a revision dated **24 January 2024** (independently corroborated by three domains' web searches and by this session's own WebSearch, though bapm.org itself could not be fetched directly by any domain — see limitations) — is scoped **explicitly to term infants only**; it does not extrapolate a threshold to preterm infants, it is simply silent on them. Its own numbers (via the verified secondary source Giouleka et al. 2023, PMID 37508719) show an internal tension worth carrying forward: a stated therapeutic goal of >2.0 mmol/L (36 mg/dL) that sits *below* the ≥2.6 mmol/L (47 mg/dL) treat-to target the Framework is usually said to be informed by (the CHYLD/Sugar Babies trial evidence). "Persistent"/"prolonged" hypoglycaemia is a distinct category with its own, higher target (>3.0 mmol/L) and a **48-hour**, not 72-hour, boundary. The NNRD real-world evaluation (Nezafat Maldonado 2026, PMID 41338963) shows a genuine, measurable admission-rate benefit (4.9→3.3/1000 term live births) without evidence of harm from delayed escalation.

### Domain B — Point-of-care glucometer vs laboratory confirmation (`domain-B-poc.md`)
The clearest single number in the entire synthesis: **BAPM states all point-of-care readings ≤2.6 mmol/L (47 mg/dL) require blood gas/laboratory confirmation** before escalation; above that, a point-of-care reading is actionable alone. This figure is not itself derived from a device-accuracy study — the accuracy literature independently derives higher confirmation cutoffs (2.8–3.5 mmol/L/50–63 mg/dL depending on device), and the UK's own MHRA device-safety guidance is more conservative still (states meters are only reliable above 3 mmol/L/54 mg/dL) — a genuine, source-documented tension between two UK bodies. Electrochemical hexokinase/glucose-dehydrogenase ("StatStrip-class") devices outperform older photometric glucose-oxidase devices, particularly at low glucose and extreme haematocrit. Continuous glucose monitoring is a distinct technology (interstitial, trend-focused) with poor sensitivity (0.39) for discrete hypoglycaemic episodes and must not be treated as a diagnostic substitute. No source located anywhere in this synthesis specifies whether governance/audit datasets should record point-of-care or laboratory-confirmed values — a genuine, unaddressed gap.

### Domain C — BAPM 48-hour term pathway, evidence grading, and the permissive-hypoglycaemia controversy (`domain-C-bapm-pathway.md`)
Grades every element of the BAPM pathway against its evidence base (full table reproduced in Part 2 below). The treatment threshold and dextrose-gel-as-treatment are RCT-supported (CHYLD, Sugar Babies); screening criteria, feeding-based first-line management, and discharge criteria are consensus/operational only; **escalation to IV dextrose — arguably the highest-stakes single step, since it triggers admission and mother-infant separation — rests on only 6 studies/711 infants with very-low-certainty evidence throughout**, the weakest-evidenced element in the whole pathway. The "cause for concern" commentary named in the brief could not be verified by PMID/DOI despite extensive attempts (see could-not-verify.md) and is not cited; the permissive-hypoglycaemia controversy is instead documented in full using two verified 1988 UK papers (Koh, PMID 3202642; Lucas, PMID 2462455) for the conservative/"undertreats" position and the CHYLD/Alsweiler evidence for the reassurance position — presented as genuinely unresolved. Treatment and prophylaxis dextrose gel have **opposite current Cochrane conclusions** (treatment: "probably effective and safe first-line"; prophylaxis: "should not be incorporated into routine practice") and must not be conflated.

### Domain D — Preterm-specific thresholds and outcomes (`domain-D-preterm.md`)
**Central finding, stated plainly per the brief's instruction: no RCT testing a specific glucose treatment threshold against long-term neurodevelopmental outcome exists for infants <32 weeks, and none exists for <28 weeks either** — confirmed across ~247 PubMed hits and 40 Consensus results, independently corroborated by the Cochrane CGM review's own statement that none of its four pooled trials reported neurodevelopmental outcome. What exists: a 26-year-old positive cohort (Duvanel 1999, PMID 10190926, recurrence-driven adverse outcome in SGA preterm infants) and two large, modern, methodologically stronger companion cohorts (Palazzo 2024 PMID 39707054 and Palazzo/Correani 2025 PMID 40609275) testing the same <32-week population and returning **null** for early (first-6-hour) hypoglycaemia — given equal prominence to Duvanel, not subordinated to it. An active preterm CGM/variability RCT literature (Galderisi 2017, REACT 2021, Battaglini 2026) demonstrably improves glycaemic exposure metrics but has not yet shown a neurodevelopmental benefit. Every glucose threshold currently applied to a <32-week infant in UK practice is an extrapolation without direct RCT support.

### Domain E — Term/late-preterm neurodevelopmental outcomes (`domain-E-term-outcomes.md`)
The CHYLD/Sugar Babies/hPOD programme (New Zealand, late-preterm/term infants at risk of hypoglycaemia): treating hypoglycaemia to ≥2.6 mmol/L is **not** associated with excess combined neurosensory impairment at 2, 4.5, or 9–10 years (McKinlay 2015 PMID 26465984; McKinlay 2017 PMID 28783802; Shah 2022 JAMA PMID 35315886), but severe, recurrent, or clinically undetected (interstitial-only) hypoglycaemia carries a dose-dependent risk of low executive function and visual-motor impairment. Dextrose gel treatment does not alter neurosensory impairment but carries a **replicated adverse visual-processing secondary signal at 4.5 and 9–10 years** (Harris 2022 PMID 35940872; St Clair 2022 PMID 36516806); prophylactic dextrose gel (hPOD) showed an adverse motor/cognitive secondary signal at 2 years (Edwards 2022 JAMA PMID 35315885) that the trial's own authors say may reflect true under-detected harm, not a reassuring null. This entire evidence base is late-preterm/term (≥32–35 weeks); none of it is direct evidence for very/extremely preterm infants.

### Domain F — Hypoglycaemic seizure and MRI (`domain-F-mri-seizure.md`)
Burns et al. 2008 (PMID 18595988, Hammersmith Hospital — the same trust as this synthesis) remains the central and only UK source: MRI pattern (white matter injury severity, PLIC involvement) **outperformed glucose severity/duration** for predicting 18-month outcome, and 3/35 infants scanned for hypoglycaemic seizure had unexpected MCA territory infarctions — supporting a differential-diagnosis role for MRI independent of its prognostic role. Two studies 14 years and a continent apart (Tam 2008 PMID 18762519; Zhang 2022 PMID 36166098) converge on DWI sensitivity for the classic injury signature being time-limited to roughly the first week, pseudonormalising thereafter — the empirical basis for scanning early. **Working recommendation: any infant with a clinical seizure attributed to neonatal hypoglycaemia warrants brain MRI, ideally within the first week**, for both prognosis and differential diagnosis — explicitly flagged as extrapolated from small, heterogeneous, largely non-UK case series rather than a guideline (no BAPM/NICE source addresses this question at all).

### Domain G — Hyperglycaemia and long-term follow-up (`domain-G-hyperglycaemia.md`)
No BAPM or NICE guidance exists on a neonatal hyperglycaemia threshold — this is a confirmed absence, not a search failure. Three distinct clinical scenarios are kept separate throughout: primary/stress hyperglycaemia in extremely/very preterm infants (Zamir 2021, PMID 33863775: duration-dependent dose-response, −0.33 FSIQ points/day), PN-related hyperglycaemia (Boscarino 2021, PMID 34199741), and secondary hyperglycaemia in neonatal encephalopathy (Puzone 2023 SR/MA, PMID 37316160: OR 3.07 for death/neurodisability — the largest effect size in the domain, but a mechanistically distinct population). **The brief's named "COIN trial" does not exist**; the correctly-named pivotal RCT is NIRTURE (Beardsall 2008, PMID 18971490), stopped early after early insulin reduced hyperglycaemia but increased hypoglycaemia and signalled higher mortality — no long-term follow-up of that cohort was ever published. Glycaemic variability/lability, prominently billed in the secondary review literature, is **not confirmed** as an independent predictor once adjusted for gestation/growth (Tottman 2017, PMID 28647271) or when directly tested by RCT (Battaglini 2026, PMID 41984740, null on neurodevelopmental outcome despite successfully reducing instability).

### §6.2 The point-of-care-versus-laboratory decision point, stated once

**A point-of-care glucose reading of ≤2.6 mmol/L (47 mg/dL) requires laboratory or blood gas confirmation before treatment escalation; above this figure, a point-of-care reading is actionable on its own for feeding/first-line management.** Source: BAPM Framework (via Domain B's web-search triangulation, since bapm.org itself could not be fetched directly this session). This figure is a consensus/operational choice, not a value derived from a dedicated diagnostic-accuracy study — the device-accuracy literature independently derives somewhat higher cutoffs (2.8–3.5 mmol/L/50–63 mg/dL depending on device and study), and the UK's own MHRA device-safety guidance is more conservative still, stating meters are reliable only above 3 mmol/L (54 mg/dL). This is a genuine, source-documented internal inconsistency within UK guidance, not resolved by this synthesis: **BAPM's clinical pathway is what actually governs UK practice** (it is implemented and audited via the NNRD), so 2.6 mmol/L (47 mg/dL) is the number to operationalise locally, but staff should understand it is not the same number the device-accuracy literature or the MHRA would independently derive.

### §6.4 The permissive-hypoglycaemia controversy — presented as genuinely unresolved

**The critique:** BAPM's threshold structure (an asymptomatic floor as low as 1.0–1.9 mmol/L/18–34 mg/dL before gel is even offered; a treatment goal of >2.0 mmol/L/36 mg/dL) undertreats risk relative to older, more conservative UK practice, and blurs the screening/diagnostic threshold distinction. Its verified evidentiary basis: **Koh et al. 1988** (PMID 3202642) — abnormal sensory evoked potentials below 2.6 mmol/L even in asymptomatic children — and **Lucas et al. 1988** (PMID 2462455) — a multicentre UK cohort (preterm, not term — a directness caveat the critique's proponents do not typically make explicit) in which ≥5 days of moderate hypoglycaemia produced a 3.5-fold increase in neurodevelopmental impairment, concluding "reappraisal of current management is urgently required." The specific commentary named in the original brief ("Thresholds for hypoglycaemic screening — a cause for concern?") could not be verified by PMID/DOI despite extensive attempts and is not cited (see `could-not-verify.md`); its argument is represented here via these two verified primary sources instead.

**The reassurance:** the randomised, prospective CHYLD evidence directly tests a treat-to-≥2.6-mmol/L *policy* (not the same question as "what happens near/below the threshold," which is what Koh and Lucas describe) in the exact population BAPM targets, and finds no excess *combined* neurosensory impairment at 2 or 4.5 years. Alsweiler et al. 2022 (PMID 36568425) supplies the modern middle position: hypoglycaemia screening cannot cleanly satisfy screening-test principles because neuroglycopenia has no direct diagnostic test, so blood glucose is an imperfect proxy by construction, not because BAPM chose the wrong number.

**Where this leaves the honest reader:** CHYLD's own domain-specific executive-function and visual-motor dose-response signal is a point of partial convergence, not a resolution — hypoglycaemia under the current threshold is not neurologically inert, which is exactly what the critique would predict, even though the *global* impairment the critique implies should be markedly worse was not observed. **This synthesis does not adjudicate between the two positions**, per the brief's explicit instruction.

### §6.5 Preterm evidence-gap statement

This is the single most important limitation for a Level 3 NICU to hold in mind when using this synthesis, and it applies with increasing force as gestation falls. The dominant RCT evidence base for glycaemic thresholds (CHYLD, Sugar Babies, hPOD/pre-hPOD) enrolled **late preterm and term infants from ~32–35 weeks upward** — not the very and extremely preterm infants who make up the majority of a Level 3 caseload. BAPM's own Framework is scoped explicitly to term infants and is silent, not extrapolating, on preterm infants. For the <32-week population, the evidence is a 26-year-old positive cohort (Duvanel 1999) and two large modern companion cohorts testing the identical question and returning null (Palazzo 2024, Palazzo/Correani 2025) — genuinely the best currently available evidence, but retrospective, single-region, and restricted to a first-6-hour exposure window that cannot speak to the recurrent/prolonged hypoglycaemia pattern Duvanel's positive finding turned on. **For <28 weeks specifically, no threshold-outcome study of any design — RCT, prospective cohort, or retrospective cohort — was identified.** An active and promising preterm CGM/glycaemic-variability RCT literature exists (Galderisi 2017, REACT 2021, Battaglini 2026) and demonstrably improves glycaemic exposure metrics, but by the field's own Cochrane review's explicit statement, none of the pooled trials have yet reported a neurodevelopmental outcome. Every glucose threshold currently applied to a very or extremely preterm infant in routine UK practice is, on this evidence, a downward extrapolation from term/late-preterm data — not a directly evidenced, gestation-specific standard — and this should be stated plainly in unit teaching and governance documentation rather than left implicit behind a number that looks the same as the term threshold but carries none of the same evidentiary weight.

### §6.6 MRI-in-hypoglycaemic-seizure — practical recommendation, evidence-graded

**Recommendation:** any infant with a clinical seizure attributed to (or occurring alongside) neonatal hypoglycaemia warrants brain MRI, for two distinct and both-clinically-relevant reasons: (i) **differential diagnosis** — to detect or exclude a coexisting arterial ischaemic stroke, HIE, structural lesion, or an atypical pattern warranting metabolic/genetic work-up (Burns 2008, PMID 18595988, found unexpected MCA territory infarctions in 3/35 infants scanned for hypoglycaemic seizure workup), a role that holds regardless of biochemical severity; and (ii) **prognostication** — MRI pattern (white matter injury severity, posterior limb of the internal capsule involvement) outperformed glucose severity/duration for predicting 18-month outcome in the same, only UK, cohort. **Timing:** scan as early as practicable, ideally within the first week — DWI sensitivity for the characteristic occipital/parieto-occipital signature declines sharply beyond roughly 6–7 days (Tam 2008, PMID 18762519; Zhang 2022, PMID 36166098; concordant findings 14 years and a continent apart), after which conventional T1/T2 sequences, not DWI, carry the diagnostic information.

**GRADE and directness, stated plainly:** the central prognostic claim carries **Moderate** certainty but rests on a single UK cohort of 35 infants with no possible comparator arm (Burns 2008); the timing recommendation carries **Low** certainty, resting on two concordant but small non-UK studies; the "scan everyone, not just the most severe" triage position is a **reasoned extrapolation from the evidence's own logic** (Burns 2008 found imaging outperformed biochemical severity specifically because biochemical severity was a poor discriminator), not a validated threshold from any trial or guideline. **No BAPM or NICE document addresses this question at all.** One promising 2025 guideline-tier source exists (Martínez-Biarge et al., Spanish Neonatal Brain Group, first author Hammersmith/Imperial College Healthcare NHS Trust-affiliated, PMID 41022518) but its specific hypoglycaemia-timing recommendation could not be extracted beyond the abstract this session (no PMC full text available) — obtaining this in full text is the single highest-value next step before writing this recommendation into formal unit guidance as anything more authoritative than an evidence-informed working position.

### §6.7 Hyperglycaemia follow-up population list

Four groups, with their supporting evidence, and one candidate group not currently confirmed:

1. **Extremely and very preterm infants with sustained, recurrent, or PN-related hyperglycaemia** — Zamir et al. 2021 (PMID 33863775, EXPRESS cohort, <27 weeks): duration-dependent dose-response, −0.33 FSIQ points per day of hyperglycaemia >8 mmol/L (144 mg/dL); Boscarino et al. 2021 (PMID 34199741): PN-related hyperglycaemia (>180 mg/dL/10.0 mmol/L) independently associated with motor delay after adjustment; van der Lugt et al. 2010 (PMID 20646308): markedly higher mortality (41% vs 8%) and, among survivors, more frequent neurological/behavioural abnormality — flagged with a survivor-bias caveat given the scale of the mortality difference.
2. **Any infant who received insulin therapy for neonatal hyperglycaemia, regardless of gestation** — not because harm is confirmed, but because reassuring long-term data are absent: the pivotal RCT (Beardsall/NIRTURE 2008, PMID 18971490) was stopped early after signalling higher 28-day mortality and increased hypoglycaemia, and no long-term neurodevelopmental follow-up of that cohort was ever published. Zamir separately found insulin exposure itself unassociated with outcome either way, which does not resolve the question.
3. **Infants with neonatal encephalopathy who develop secondary hyperglycaemia** — Puzone et al. 2023 (PMID 37316160, PROSPERO CRD42022368870): the largest pooled effect size anywhere in this domain (OR 3.07, 95% CI 2.17–4.35, for death/neurodisability ≥18 months) — but this population already warrants follow-up for its primary NE diagnosis, and hyperglycaemia should be understood as a marker that sharpens surveillance intensity, not a separately-caused injury requiring its own distinct referral pathway.
4. **Candidate, not confirmed: infants with high glycaemic variability/instability independent of any single threshold breach.** Tottman et al. 2017 (PMID 28647271) found this did not survive adjustment for gestation/growth restriction; Battaglini et al. 2026 (PMID 41984740), the one RCT that successfully reduced glycaemic instability via CGM, found no neurodevelopmental benefit at 2 years (albeit in a trial almost certainly underpowered for that outcome specifically). This remains a research question, not an established follow-up indication, notwithstanding its prominent billing in the secondary review literature (Lagacé & Tam 2024, PMID 38972961).

**No BAPM or NICE guidance exists for a neonatal hyperglycaemia threshold at all** — a unit using a local numeric trigger (commonly 8–10 mmol/L/144–180 mg/dL) is following reasonable extrapolation from the observational literature and physiological reasoning, not a national standard, and this should be stated as such rather than presented as guideline-derived.

## Known limitations of this synthesis, stated plainly

1. **bapm.org itself was never directly retrieved.** Every BAPM-specific number in this synthesis is triangulated from peer-reviewed secondary characterisations (Giouleka 2023, Rusu 2026) and web-search-indexed snippets/NHS trust guidelines citing BAPM verbatim, not from the primary document's text. This session's own attempt to fetch bapm.org directly (from the parent context, not just the subagents) also returned `EGRESS_BLOCKED`, confirming this is an environment-wide network policy rather than an agent-specific failure. The 24 January 2024 revision date is independently corroborated across three domains and a direct WebSearch, which supports confidence in that specific fact, but individual numeric thresholds should be re-verified against bapm.org directly the moment access is available.
2. **One named commentary could not be verified and is excluded** ("Thresholds for hypoglycaemic screening — a cause for concern?") — its argument is represented via alternative verified sources, per citation-integrity rules, but the specific piece itself is not cited anywhere.
3. **The extraction matrix required manual reconstruction for three domains.** The parallel research agents for Domains C, D, and F produced CSV files with unescaped-comma quoting defects that misaligned columns; rather than risk silently transcribing misaligned data, those rows were rebuilt by hand from the verified prose in the domain synthesis files, which does not have this problem. The domain synthesis `.md` files, not the individual `domain-C/D/F.csv` files (retained for audit trail only), are the authoritative source for those three domains' extracted data — `02-extraction/extraction.csv` (the merged, corrected master) should be used for any onward analysis.
