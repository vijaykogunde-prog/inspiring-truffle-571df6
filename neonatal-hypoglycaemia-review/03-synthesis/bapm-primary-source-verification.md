# BAPM primary-source verification and corrections

**Status: supersedes secondary-sourced BAPM numbers throughout Domains A, B, and C.**

Throughout the original research phase, `bapm.org` was unreachable from every domain agent's network environment (`EGRESS_BLOCKED` on every attempt). BAPM's own numbers were therefore triangulated from peer-reviewed secondary characterisations (Giouleka et al. 2023, PMID 37508719; Rusu et al. 2026, PMID 42194883) and web-search-indexed NHS trust guidelines citing BAPM verbatim. This was flagged throughout as the single most important limitation of the synthesis.

**The user subsequently supplied the primary document directly**: *Identification and Management of Neonatal Hypoglycaemia in the Full-Term Infant (Birth – 72 hours) — A BAPM Framework for Practice*, January 2024 (with amendments 20/06/2024 and 16/09/2024), chaired by Dr Frances O'Brien (2023 revision) and Professor James Boardman (2017). This document was read in full and is now the authoritative source for every BAPM-specific claim below. This addendum documents every material correction. **This is a governance-relevant finding in its own right**: several numbers reported earlier in this synthesis, though carefully triangulated and internally consistent across independent secondary sources, differ from the primary document. This is recorded transparently rather than silently corrected in place, because the earlier (incorrect) figures were provisionally cited in `03-synthesis/domain-A-definitions.md`, `domain-B-poc.md`, `domain-C-bapm-pathway.md`, and the plain-language threshold table — all now superseded by this addendum for anything numeric.

## 1. The corrected BAPM operational thresholds (verified primary text, January 2024 revision)

BAPM's current operational threshold approach (Practice Point 6) is **not** anchored on 2.6 mmol/L (47 mg/dL) as this synthesis previously stated. It is a three-tier structure:

1. **<1.0 mmol/L (18 mg/dL) at any time** → urgent (severe).
2. **A single value <2.5 mmol/L (45 mg/dL) in an infant with abnormal clinical signs** → urgent.
3. **More than two measurements <2.0 mmol/L (36 mg/dL) in a baby with a risk factor but no abnormal clinical signs** → intervene.

**Dextrose gel** (Practice Point 7): buccal 40% gel, 200 mg/kg (≈0.5 mL/kg — see Table 2 of the Framework for exact weight-band volumes), may be used alongside a feeding plan when BG is **1.0–1.9 mmol/L (18–34 mg/dL)**, up to 2 doses 30 minutes apart per episode, maximum 6 doses in 48 hours; clinical examination advised before the 3rd dose.

**Severe/persistent** (Practice Point 8, and see §2 below on an internal 48h/72h inconsistency in the document itself): BG <1.0 mmol/L (severe) or >2 measurements <2.0 mmol/L in the first 48 hours (persistent) → urgent medical review and investigation.

**First BG check**: before the 2nd feed, **2–4 hours** after birth (not a flat "4 hours" as previously stated).

**Discharge criteria**: minimum 24 hours of age AND **2 consecutive** pre-feed BG measurements >2.0 mmol/L (not 3, as this synthesis previously stated) AND effective feeding established.

**Hyperinsulinism/suspected CHI target — direction corrected**: maintain BG **>3.0 mmol/L in the first 48 hours**, rising to **>3.5 mmol/L after 48 hours**. This synthesis previously had the direction backwards (it stated the target "rises to >3.0 mmol/L beyond 48h" — the primary text has 3.0 mmol/L as the *earlier*, lower-acuity figure and 3.5 mmol/L as the *later*, escalated one).

**HIE**: operational threshold raised to **at least 2.5 mmol/L** in infants with moderate–severe hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy.

**IV escalation (Flowchart C)** — this synthesis's inferred figures were correct: IV access, bloods (glucose, insulin/C-peptide, free fatty acids, beta-hydroxybutyrate prioritised; urine ketones/organic acids), admit to neonatal unit; **IV 10% glucose bolus 2.5 mL/kg + infusion at 60 mL/kg/day**; recheck at 30 minutes; if still <1.0 mmol/L or abnormal signs, repeat bolus and increase glucose delivery rate by 2 mg/kg/min; if glucose infusion rate exceeds 8 mg/kg/min, suspect hyperinsulinism and raise the threshold to 3.0 mmol/L (3.5 mmol/L beyond 48h). Interim bridge while awaiting IV access: buccal dextrose gel 200 mg/kg **or** IM glucagon 200 micrograms/kg (max 1 mg).

**Formula volumes** (unchanged from what this synthesis previously reported): 40–60 mL/kg/day (0–24h), 70–90 mL/kg/day (24–48h), 100–120 mL/kg/day (48–72h).

## 2. An inconsistency within the primary document itself, reported rather than resolved

The Framework's own text is not fully internally consistent on the time window for "persistent" hypoglycaemia: Practice Point 8 and the "Persistent or recurrent" section header (line ~893 of the source PDF) both anchor persistent/recurrent hypoglycaemia to **the first 48 hours** ("more than two measurements <2.0mmol/L in the first 48 hours"), while Practice Points 21 and 23 anchor the same concept to **the first 72 hours** ("more than 2 BG measurements <2.0mmol/L within the 72 hours of birth"). This is reported as a genuine feature of the primary document, not a transcription error on this synthesis's part — a unit writing local guidance from this Framework should resolve which window it is adopting rather than assume the document is internally uniform.

## 3. LGA is explicitly **not** a routine risk factor in current BAPM guidance — a correction

The current (2024) risk-factor list for BG monitoring is: fetal growth restriction (birth weight <2nd centile, using the gestation/sex-specific values in the Framework's own Table 1, or clinical wasting), infants of mothers with diabetes, and infants of mothers on beta-blockers in the third trimester/at birth. **Large-for-gestational-age (LGA) is explicitly excluded from routine screening** unless there is diabetes or features suggestive of Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome. The Framework states this plainly: registry and case-series evidence for LGA as an independent risk factor is confounded by retrospective design, inconsistent devices, timing variation, and limited phenotyping, and "the working group considers that... routine screening of LGA infants for hypoglycaemia is not indicated." Domains A and C previously listed LGA alongside IDM/SGA/late-preterm as an undifferentiated standard risk factor; this should be corrected — LGA is a contested, explicitly-not-recommended-for-routine-screening factor in current guidance, not an equivalent-standing one.

Moderate-to-late preterm infants are flagged as at-risk but are explicitly **out of scope for this document** — their care is covered by a separate BAPM Framework ("early postnatal care of moderate to late preterm infants"), not this one. This reinforces, from the primary source itself, Domain A's finding that BAPM's term Framework does not extend its own numeric thresholds to preterm infants even by cross-reference.

## 4. HypoEXIT — the threshold-comparison RCT no domain agent found

**van Kempen AAMW et al. "Lower versus Traditional Treatment Threshold for Neonatal Hypoglycemia." *N Engl J Med* 2020;382:534–544. PMID 32023373, DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa1905593.** Verified this session via PubMed with full abstract.

This is a direct, adequately-powered, multicentre, randomised **noninferiority** trial — precisely the study design every domain agent's search concluded did not exist ("no randomised trial comparing different treatment thresholds with sufficiently long and complete follow-up has been carried out," in BAPM's own words, immediately before it cites this same trial — see §5). 689 otherwise-healthy newborns ≥35 weeks' gestation at risk of hypoglycaemia were randomised to a **lower threshold** (treat at <2.0 mmol/L, 36 mg/dL) versus a **traditional threshold** (treat at <2.6 mmol/L, 47 mg/dL) for asymptomatic moderate hypoglycaemia, with the primary outcome psychomotor development at 18 months (Bayley-III-NL). 287/348 (82.5%) and 295/341 (86.5%) were assessed respectively. Cognitive and motor scores were closely similar between groups (cognitive 102.9±0.7 vs 102.2±0.7; motor 104.6±0.7 vs 104.9±0.7) and the **prespecified noninferiority margin (7.5 points, half an SD) was not crossed** — the lower threshold was noninferior. The traditional (higher) threshold group had fewer and less severe hypoglycaemic episodes but **more invasive diagnostic and treatment interventions**; length of stay was similar. Serious adverse events in the lower-threshold group included one case of convulsions during normoglycaemia and one death — reported here for completeness and with the same "give nulls and adverse signals full prominence" instruction that governs the rest of this synthesis, though the trial does not attribute these to the lower threshold specifically and the numbers are too small to interpret as a signal either way.

**Directness and GRADE**: direct evidence for the ≥35-week at-risk population (same population as CHYLD/Sugar Babies). **GRADE: Moderate** — randomised, adequately powered for its noninferiority design, masked assessment; downgraded for imprecision (noninferiority margin is a pre-specified clinical threshold, not the tightest possible bound) and for being a single trial not yet independently replicated.

**Why this matters for the permissive-hypoglycaemia controversy (§6.4 of the cross-cutting synthesis)**: this is materially different evidence from what that section previously had available. It is not simply reassuring association data (like CHYLD's treat-to-threshold cohort) — it is a **head-to-head randomised comparison of two specific numeric thresholds**, and it found the *lower* one (2.0 mmol/L) was not worse. This directly undercuts the strongest form of the "permissive hypoglycaemia undertreats" critique (which argues the threshold itself is dangerously low) with the one study design capable of actually answering that specific question, while still leaving the Koh 1988/Lucas 1988 evidence and CHYLD's own domain-specific executive-function/visual-motor dose-response signal as genuine, unresolved counterweights. The controversy should now be presented as: **the critique is not supported by the one adequately-powered RCT that directly tests the threshold question, but it is not thereby closed**, because HypoEXIT tested asymptomatic moderate hypoglycaemia specifically (not severe, recurrent, or symptomatic episodes, which is exactly the higher-risk subgroup CHYLD's dose-response findings and the Koh/Lucas evidence concern).

## 5. BAPM's own stated rationale for **not** adopting 2.6 mmol/L — verbatim

The Framework is explicit that it considered and rejected changing to a CHYLD/HypoEXIT-style 2.6 mmol/L threshold: *"In summary, no study has shown that treatment of asymptomatic hypoglycaemia in 'at risk' groups improves neurodevelopmental outcome... In the absence of these forms of evidence the group considered that there is no new argument to support a change in the operational threshold approach proposed by Cornblath et al in 2000."* It also offers its own critical reading of CHYLD, worth quoting because it is BAPM's own methodological caution, not this synthesis's: *"the mean difference in BG concentration between those who did and those who did not have a poor outcome was only 0.16mmol/L. These observations raise the possibility that adverse outcomes... may be a function of the underlying risk profile rather than BG concentration per se."* This is a more skeptical reading of CHYLD's reassurance than this synthesis's Domain E/C sections previously conveyed, and should be incorporated as BAPM's own stated position, not merely this synthesis's independent literature reading.

## 6. Point-of-care confirmation — corrected

Domain B's synthesis stated, as an ostensibly direct BAPM quotation, that "all low values (≤2.6 mmol/L) require confirmation using blood gas analysis." **This exact figure and framing could not be located anywhere in the verified primary text and should be treated as a misattribution, not a confirmed BAPM position.** What the Framework actually says (Practice Points 3–6, Appendix 5): **blood gas analysers should be considered the reference standard** for BG measurement (reporting a calculated "plasma glucose equivalent"), and **"many handheld glucose meters are not sufficiently accurate in the range of 0–2.0 mmol/L"** — a range statement, not a single confirmation cut-off. The Framework does not specify a single numeric point-of-care value below which laboratory/blood gas confirmation is mandatory; it instead requires that any POCT device's performance be locally validated with pathology/Healthcare Scientist input before use, particularly at low glucose values and high haematocrit. **The single clearest "confirm below X" figure claimed in the original Domain B synthesis should therefore be removed from the cross-cutting synthesis** and replaced with: BAPM designates blood gas analysis as the reference standard throughout, treats meter accuracy in the 0–2.0 mmol/L range as a general caution rather than a specific confirmatory threshold, and leaves the exact operational confirmation rule to local validation — which is a materially different, and more cautious, position than a single stated number.

## 7. Sugar Babies — the original 2013 acute-outcome RCT, now separately verified

**Harris DL, Weston PJ, Signal M, Chase JG, Harding JE. "Dextrose gel for neonatal hypoglycaemia (the Sugar Babies Study): a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial." *Lancet* 2013;382(9910):2077–2083. PMID 24075361, DOI 10.1016/S0140-6736(13)61645-1.** Verified this session with full abstract. This is the foundational acute-outcome trial underlying the 2-year, 4.5-year, and 9–10-year follow-ups already fully extracted in Domain E (rows E6–E8) — it was previously cited only via those follow-ups and the Cochrane review, not extracted as its own primary row. 237 babies (35–42 weeks, <48h old) randomised 1:1 to 40% dextrose gel 200 mg/kg or placebo. Primary outcome, treatment failure (BG <2.6 mmol/L after two treatment attempts): **16/118 (14%) dextrose vs 29/119 (24%) placebo, relative risk 0.57, 95% CI 0.33–0.98, p=0.04.** No serious adverse events. This RR 0.57 figure is the trial's own primary acute-outcome estimate and is distinct from, and should not be confused with, the Cochrane review's separately pooled "neurosensory disability" estimate (RR 6.27, very wide CI) already reported in Domain C's C5 extraction row, which is a different, much rarer, longer-term outcome pooled across two trials.
