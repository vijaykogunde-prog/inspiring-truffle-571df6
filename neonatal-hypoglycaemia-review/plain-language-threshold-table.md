# Neonatal Glucose Thresholds — Quick Reference

*For pinning to a unit guideline or teaching slide. Full evidence and citations: `03-synthesis/synthesis.md` §6.1 and `03-synthesis/bapm-primary-source-verification.md`. All values: mmol/L (mg/dL); 1 mmol/L = 18.0 mg/dL. BAPM figures verified directly against the primary Framework document (January 2024, amended 20/06/2024 and 16/09/2024).*

**Remember throughout: the number below is when we act, not the number at which harm is proven to occur (screening/action threshold ≠ injury threshold).**

**BAPM's own operational threshold is a tiered 1.0 / 2.0 / 2.5 mmol/L system — not 2.6 mmol/L.** The 2.6 mmol/L (47 mg/dL) figure belongs to the CHYLD/Sugar Babies/HypoEXIT trial programme, which BAPM cites as reassuring evidence for *not* changing its own lower thresholds, not as the number it has itself adopted. Keep these two numbers distinct when teaching.

## The one sentence to remember for each population

- **Term, 0–48h:** BAPM's own tiered threshold (1.0/2.0/2.5 mmol/L) is real-world proven to reduce unnecessary admissions and is explicitly *not* the 2.6 mmol/L CHYLD figure — BAPM considered and declined to adopt 2.6 mmol/L, citing insufficient new evidence to change from Cornblath's original operational-threshold approach. HypoEXIT now provides direct RCT evidence that an even lower threshold (2.0 mmol/L) is not worse than 2.6 mmol/L at 18 months — but domain-specific (executive function, visual-motor) risk from CHYLD's dose-response data is not zero, especially with severe, recurrent, or clinically undetected episodes.
- **Term, >48h (or >72h, per the other practice points in the same document):** this is a different disease category (persistent hypoglycaemia) with a different target and a different work-up — do not manage it on the same pathway as the first 48 hours.
- **Late preterm (34–36+6 wk):** treated the same as term by the trial evidence that actually included this group — this is genuine, direct RCT-grade reassurance, not extrapolation. BAPM's own Framework does not cover this group at all (separate document).
- **Very and extremely preterm (<32 wk, especially <28 wk):** every number applied here is borrowed from term/late-preterm evidence. Say so out loud. The best modern preterm-specific evidence (two 2024/25 Italian cohorts) is reassuring but only tested the first 6 hours of life and cannot speak to recurrent or prolonged hypoglycaemia later in the NICU stay.
- **Hyperglycaemia, any preterm gestation:** there is no national number to defer to, and the one adequately randomised attempt to fix it with insulin (NIRTURE) found a harm signal, not a benefit — treat cautiously, and do not assume correcting the glucose number itself will improve outcome.
- **Point-of-care vs lab:** BAPM does not state a single confirmation number — blood gas is the reference standard throughout, and meters are flagged as generally less reliable in the 0–2.0 mmol/L range. Local device validation with pathology/Healthcare Scientist input governs the exact local rule.
- **Hypoglycaemic seizure:** get an MRI, ideally within the first week — it tells you more than the glucose number does, both for prognosis and for ruling out something else (stroke, HIE) hiding behind the hypoglycaemia story.
- **LGA:** BAPM's current guidance explicitly does **not** recommend routine hypoglycaemia screening for LGA infants alone (birthweight >90th centile) absent maternal diabetes or Beckwith-Wiedemann features — the registry/case-series evidence for LGA as an independent risk factor is considered too confounded to act on routinely.
