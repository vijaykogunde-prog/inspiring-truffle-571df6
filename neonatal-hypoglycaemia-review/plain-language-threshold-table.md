# Neonatal Glucose Thresholds — Quick Reference

*For pinning to a unit guideline or teaching slide. Full evidence and citations: `03-synthesis/synthesis.md` §6.1. All values: mmol/L (mg/dL); 1 mmol/L = 18.0 mg/dL.*

**Remember throughout: the number below is when we act, not the number at which harm is proven to occur (screening/action threshold ≠ injury threshold).**

| Gestation | Age | What to do | Threshold | Where this number comes from | How solid is the evidence for THIS gestation? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Term** (≥37 wk) | 0–4h | Normal transitional dip — do not treat on this alone | as low as **1.6 mmol/L (30 mg/dL)** can be physiological | BAPM | Guidance, not a trial number |
| **Term** | 4–48h, asymptomatic | Feed + 40% dextrose gel | **1.0–1.9 mmol/L (18–34 mg/dL)** | BAPM | Guidance |
| **Term** | 4–48h | Escalate to IV dextrose | **<2.0 mmol/L (36 mg/dL)** after initial treatment, or **<2.5 mmol/L (45 mg/dL)** if symptomatic, or **<1.0 mmol/L (18 mg/dL)** at any time | BAPM | Guidance — the IV-dextrose step itself has very-low-certainty trial support (only 6 studies/711 infants worldwide) |
| **Term/late preterm** | 4–48h | **Treat-to target** — the number backed by a real trial | **≥2.6 mmol/L (47 mg/dL)** | CHYLD trial (McKinlay 2015/2017) | **Strong** — RCT-grade, this exact population |
| **Term** | Point-of-care reading | Confirm with blood gas/lab before escalating | **≤2.6 mmol/L (47 mg/dL)** | BAPM | Guidance — note MHRA (device safety) is stricter, saying meters are only reliable **above 3 mmol/L (54 mg/dL)** |
| **All term** | Beyond 48h — "persistent"/"prolonged" | Different problem now — critical sample, endocrine referral | **<2.6 mmol/L (47 mg/dL)** on >3 occasions; target rises to **>3.0 mmol/L** | BAPM | Guidance; distinct differential diagnosis (hyperinsulinism, metabolic, endocrine) |
| **Late preterm** (34–36+6 wk) | 0–72h | Same as term | Same ≥2.6 mmol/L (47 mg/dL) target | CHYLD/Sugar Babies (this band was actually enrolled) | **Strong for this band specifically** |
| **Moderate preterm** (32–33+6 wk) | 0–72h | Extrapolated from term/late-preterm | Same ≥2.6 mmol/L (47 mg/dL) | Extrapolation | **Weak** — partial trial overlap at best, verify exact trial cutoff |
| **Very preterm** (28–31+6 wk) | 0–72h | Extrapolated — no dedicated RCT | Same ≥2.6 mmol/L (47 mg/dL) in practice | Extrapolation from term data + one old (1999) and two modern (2024/25) preterm cohorts | **Weak** — best modern evidence (Palazzo 2024/2025) is a **null** for early hypoglycaemia, but only tested the first 6 hours |
| **Extremely preterm** (<28 wk) | 0–72h | Extrapolated — **no threshold-outcome study of any kind exists** | Same ≥2.6 mmol/L (47 mg/dL) in practice, by convention only | None — this is a stated evidence gap | **No direct evidence exists.** State this plainly to trainees. |
| **Extremely/very preterm** | first week | Hyperglycaemia — no national guidance | commonly **8–10 mmol/L (144–180 mg/dL)** used locally | Local consensus only — **no BAPM or NICE threshold exists** | Observational associations only; the one RCT of treating it (insulin, NIRTURE) showed a harm signal, not benefit |
| **Term/near-term with neonatal encephalopathy** | acute | Both hypo- and hyperglycaemia matter here — different population | n/a — treat per NE pathway, monitor closely | Puzone 2023 meta-analysis | Direct for NE; **do not** apply to primary preterm hyperglycaemia or vice versa |

## The one sentence to remember for each population

- **Term, 0–48h:** BAPM's pathway is real-world proven to reduce unnecessary admissions, and the ≥2.6 mmol/L treat-to target has genuine randomised-trial reassurance behind it (no excess *global* impairment) — but domain-specific (executive function, visual-motor) risk is not zero, especially with severe, recurrent, or clinically undetected episodes.
- **Term, >48h:** this is a different disease category (persistent hypoglycaemia) with a different target and a different work-up — do not manage it on the same pathway as the first 48 hours.
- **Late preterm (34–36+6 wk):** treated the same as term by the trial evidence that actually included this group — this is genuine, direct RCT-grade reassurance, not extrapolation.
- **Very and extremely preterm (<32 wk, especially <28 wk):** every number applied here is borrowed from term/late-preterm evidence. Say so out loud. The best modern preterm-specific evidence (two 2024/25 Italian cohorts) is reassuring but only tested the first 6 hours of life and cannot speak to recurrent or prolonged hypoglycaemia later in the NICU stay.
- **Hyperglycaemia, any preterm gestation:** there is no national number to defer to, and the one adequately randomised attempt to fix it with insulin (NIRTURE) found a harm signal, not a benefit — treat cautiously, and do not assume correcting the glucose number itself will improve outcome.
- **Point-of-care vs lab:** ≤2.6 mmol/L (47 mg/dL) → confirm with blood gas/lab before escalating. Above that, act on the meter reading — do not wait for a lab result.
- **Hypoglycaemic seizure:** get an MRI, ideally within the first week — it tells you more than the glucose number does, both for prognosis and for ruling out something else (stroke, HIE) hiding behind the hypoglycaemia story.
