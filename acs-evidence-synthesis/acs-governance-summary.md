# Antenatal Corticosteroids — Governance Summary
*Two-page briefing for the departmental meeting. Full evidence in `acs-evidence-synthesis.md`; draft guideline in `acs-guideline-draft.md`.*

## Why this review happened

A clinician raised a concern that the unit's use of 12-hourly dexamethasone dosing might be non-standard and possibly linked to worse neurodevelopmental outcome, believing 24-hourly to be the recommended interval. This review was commissioned to check that premise against the evidence before any practice change was considered, and to answer six related operational questions (agent choice, compressed dosing near delivery, chorioamnionitis, mixed and incomplete courses, and long-term outcomes) that arise in routine unit practice.

## What the evidence actually shows

**The premise was inverted.** Twelve-hourly dexamethasone (6 mg IM × 4, 24 mg total) is the internationally preferred, most trial-tested dexamethasone regimen — endorsed by WHO (2022) and ACOG, and offered as a first-listed RCOG option. It is betamethasone, not dexamethasone, that is conventionally given 24-hourly, because only the betamethasone preparation contains a slow-release depot component. **No trial has ever compared 12-hourly vs 24-hourly dexamethasone with neurodevelopmental follow-up** — this is a genuine evidence gap, not a demonstrated risk, and current unit practice does not require correction on this basis.

Beyond the premise check, the review surfaced a genuinely important, previously under-discussed finding: **the one long-term-outcome comparison that remains statistically unresolved is not dosing interval, but agent choice's effect on cerebral palsy risk** (RR 2.50, 95% CI 0.97–6.39, from the only adequately powered head-to-head trial's pooled analysis) — a single trial with a wide confidence interval that cannot exclude an important increase in risk with dexamethasone, nor confirm one. This is worth the department's attention independent of the original interval question.

## What should change

1. **Correct the internal narrative:** stop treating 12-hourly dexamethasone dosing as a deviation requiring justification. Communicate this clearly to staff who may share the original misconception.
2. **Adopt explicit documentation standards** for incomplete and mixed-agent courses (see guideline draft §6/§10) — currently likely undocumented as distinct categories, based on the case that prompted this review.
3. **Reinforce that a dose should never be withheld because delivery seems imminent**, including within the final hour before birth — the evidence (though limited) shows no harm and possible benefit from dosing this close to delivery, and the best trial evidence on omitting a dose could not show that omission is safe.
4. **Reinforce that chorioamnionitis is not a reason to withhold ACS** if delivery is not imminent — current three-guideline consensus (RCOG/NICE/WHO, as reconstructed from secondary sources) treats infection as a timing caution, not a contraindication, and the observational evidence consistently supports continued benefit.
5. **Update the parent-counselling script** for the "born at term after all" question to reflect genuinely mixed, not one-sided, evidence (Finnish register data showing an association; a Taiwanese cohort showing none) — see the parent counselling aid for suggested wording.

## What should not change

- **Agent choice.** No superiority of dexamethasone or betamethasone is established for the primary composite outcome; current local formulary-driven choice is reasonable, provided the cerebral-palsy uncertainty (above) is disclosed rather than glossed over.
- **The standard 2-dose (24-hourly) or 4-dose (12-hourly) regimens themselves.** No accelerated or compressed alternative has trial support.
- **Repeat-course policy** (maximum two courses, ≥7 days apart) — current UK guidance is consistent with the evidence, with a specific caution (below) about pregnancies that may end at term.

## A specific new caution surfaced by this review

The clearest harm signal anywhere in the repeat-course literature is **not** in the overall repeat-course population (5-year outcome OR 1.02, no difference) but is **concentrated specifically in babies who were ultimately born at term** after a repeat course (OR 1.69, 95% CI 1.04–2.77). This is a useful, evidence-based refinement to raise with the obstetric team when a repeat course is being considered in a pregnancy that may not, in the end, deliver preterm.

## Genuine evidence gaps (not failures of this review)

- No dedicated trial of dexamethasone dosing interval with neurodevelopmental follow-up exists — could be a candidate for future collaborative research, though likely low priority given the absence of any current safety signal.
- No RCT has ever enrolled women with active chorioamnionitis to test ACS directly in that population — unlikely to be remedied, given the ethics of withholding a treatment already known to benefit generally.
- No clinical data exist on mixed-agent courses at all — a genuinely novel finding of "nothing has ever been published on this," relevant given the case that prompted this review.
- The cerebral palsy signal for dexamethasone vs betamethasone (RR 2.50, wide CI) needs independent replication that does not currently exist.

## Implementation and audit plan

1. Circulate the guideline draft (`acs-guideline-draft.md`) for consultant obstetric and neonatal review; incorporate feedback; adopt formally once the flagged guideline-wording claims have been checked against primary RCOG/NICE text.
2. Brief all relevant staff (obstetric, neonatal, midwifery) on the corrected premise regarding dosing interval, to prevent inappropriate practice change based on the original misconception.
3. Update local documentation proformas to capture: agent(s), exact doses/times, course-completion status (complete/incomplete/mixed/repeat), interval from first dose to delivery, and chorioamnionitis status at dosing — the dataset needed for both counselling and the audit measures below.
4. Re-audit in 12 months against the metrics listed in the guideline draft §11 (course completion rates by gestation, mixed/incomplete course documentation compliance, repeat-course adherence, late-preterm hypoglycaemia rate, any case of ACS withheld for chorioamnionitis).
5. Update the parent information leaflet to the version in `acs-parent-counselling-aid.md`, or equivalent, ensuring the term-exposure question is answered with the same honesty reflected there.
