# Antenatal Corticosteroids — Unit Guideline (Draft)

*Draft for departmental review. Every recommendation below carries a certainty label and a reference number pointing to `acs-evidence-synthesis.md`. This draft should not be adopted until the primary RCOG GTG74 and NICE NG25 documents have been independently checked against the guideline-wording claims flagged "WebSearch-summary" throughout the synthesis — see that document's opening note and "Could not verify" list.*

---

## 1. Premise statement (read this first)

**12-hourly dexamethasone dosing (6 mg IM × 4) is the internationally preferred, most trial-tested regimen** — endorsed by WHO (2022) and ACOG, and offered as a first-listed option by RCOG GTG74. It is **not** a deviation from standard practice. Betamethasone, not dexamethasone, is the drug conventionally given 24-hourly (2 × 12 mg), because only the betamethasone sodium phosphate/acetate preparation contains a slow-release depot fraction. *[Certainty: High — synthesis §Q0, refs 1–2,14]*

No trial has ever compared 12-hourly vs 24-hourly **dexamethasone** with any outcome, including neurodevelopment. This is an evidence gap, not a demonstrated safety difference. *[Certainty: absence-of-evidence — synthesis §Q0–Q1]*

## 2. Agent of choice and rationale

Either dexamethasone or betamethasone is acceptable as first-line therapy. The only adequately powered, blinded, long-term-follow-up head-to-head trial (ASTEROID, n=1346/1509) found no meaningful difference in death-or-neurosensory-disability at 2 years (adjRR 0.97, 95% CI 0.83–1.13). *[Certainty: Moderate — ref 10]*

**Exception/caution:** the pooled cerebral palsy estimate (RR 2.50, 95% CI 0.97–6.39, single trial) cannot exclude an important increase in risk with dexamethasone. This should be disclosed as an open question, not resolved either way, in any local agent-preference statement. *[Certainty: Low — refs 3,12,18]*

Agent selection may reasonably follow local formulary and practical considerations (preparation availability, UK-specific pharmacokinetic caveats about betamethasone-phosphate-alone) rather than a proven efficacy/safety difference. *[Certainty: guideline-consensus — synthesis §Q4]*

## 3. Exact regimen and interval

| Agent | Standard regimen | Total dose |
|---|---|---|
| **Dexamethasone** (preferred/most evidence-dense regimen) | 6 mg IM every 12 hours × 4 doses | 24 mg |
| **Dexamethasone** (RCOG-listed alternative; matches ASTEROID's tested regimen) | 12 mg IM every 24 hours × 2 doses | 24 mg |
| **Betamethasone** (sodium phosphate/acetate mix, e.g. Celestone Chronodose-type preparation) | 11.4–12 mg IM every 24 hours × 2 doses | 22.8–24 mg |

**UK-specific caution:** if the betamethasone preparation available locally is betamethasone phosphate *alone* (without the acetate depot fraction), RCOG GTG74 flags that its pharmacokinetics differ from the phosphate/acetate mix and evidence to guide its dosing is limited — confirm which preparation is stocked before assuming the standard 24-hourly interval applies. *[Certainty: guideline statement, evidence base thin — ref 1]*

Do not present the 12-hourly dexamethasone regimen as requiring correction to 24-hourly. *[Certainty: High]*

## 4. What to do when delivery is imminent

- **Give the first dose regardless of how imminent delivery appears.** Every study identified shows a partial/single dose reduces risk relative to no dose, with the largest relative benefit at the earliest gestations. *[Certainty: Moderate — ref 25, synthesis §Q2]*
- Benefit from ACS rises continuously with time from first dose to delivery; there is no identified interval below which benefit disappears entirely, though the largest and most consistent benefit accrues over 18–48 hours. *[Certainty: Moderate — refs 26–27]*
- **Give the scheduled second dose even if delivery is expected within the hour.** The closest available evidence (a small cohort with mean dose-to-delivery interval ≈1 hour) shows no harm and a possible benefit signal; the best RCT evidence on omitting a dose (BETADOSE) could not show that omission is safe. *[Certainty: Low for the specific ~1h scenario; Moderate-high that omission should not be routine — refs 28–29]*
- No accelerated (compressed-interval) regimen has been shown superior to the standard schedule in an imminent-delivery context, and none has been adequately trial-tested for this specific purpose. *[Certainty: absence-of-evidence]*
- No dedicated postnatal monitoring change (e.g. enhanced glucose surveillance) is supported by direct evidence for infants who received a dose very close to delivery specifically — standard preterm/ACS-exposed monitoring applies. *[Certainty: absence-of-evidence]*

## 5. What to do with chorioamnionitis

- **Chorioamnionitis (clinical or histological) should not by itself withhold ACS** if delivery is not imminent and the woman is otherwise a candidate. Every pooled analysis identified shows ACS benefit persists in this population (mortality OR ~0.45–0.51, RDS OR ~0.53–0.59, IVH OR ~0.35–0.41). *[Certainty: Low — all observational; no RCT has directly tested this population — refs 32–33]*
- **Do not delay delivery to complete an ACS course when chorioamnionitis is driving the clinical picture.** Give what course is achievable in the time available; delivery timing takes priority. *[Certainty: guideline-consensus across RCOG/WHO positions — see synthesis §Q3 caveat on WebSearch-sourced wording]*
- Maintain a low threshold for neonatal sepsis surveillance after ACS in chorioamnionitis-affected pregnancies — one small study found an increased-sepsis signal with "optimally timed" ACS in this group (aOR 6.85, wide CI), though this should not be read as a reason to withhold treatment. *[Certainty: Low, single small study — ref 34]*
- No evidence addresses whether chorioamnionitis should change the timing of a second dose specifically — this is a genuine gap; use standard-interval second dosing unless delivery intervenes. *[Certainty: absence-of-evidence]*

## 6. Classifying and documenting incomplete and mixed courses

**Incomplete course** (single dose given, course not completed before delivery or otherwise): document as such; a single dose is better than none, with benefit gestational-age-dependent (greatest at the earliest gestations). *[Certainty: Moderate — ref 25]*

**Mixed-agent course** (e.g. one dose betamethasone, one dose dexamethasone): document explicitly as **non-standard**, naming both agents, doses, and the interval between them. Pharmacological reasoning (not clinical evidence — none exists for this scenario) suggests such a course does not reproduce either drug's tested pharmacokinetic profile and should be managed clinically as equivalent to an incomplete course. *[Certainty: inference only, not graded clinical evidence — synthesis §Q5]* Suggested documentation wording: *"Mixed-agent antenatal corticosteroid course: [agent 1, dose] followed by [agent 2, dose] at [interval]. Non-standard course — pharmacokinetic profile does not match either single-agent regimen. Managed as incomplete course for clinical/counselling purposes."*

## 7. When a second dose is, and is not, worth giving

**Worth giving:** whenever a woman remains within the standard course window and delivery has not yet occurred — including when delivery is expected imminently (see §4). *[Certainty: Moderate-high — ref 29]*

**Not established as worth giving:** a "rescue"/extra dose beyond the standard 2-dose (or 4-dose) course, given close to delivery specifically to compress timing — no trial supports this as superior to standard dosing. *[Certainty: absence-of-evidence]*

**Not established as safe to omit:** the second dose of a standard course, even when it will land very close to delivery — BETADOSE could not demonstrate non-inferiority of a single dose. *[Certainty: Moderate-high — ref 29]*

## 8. Repeat-course policy

- A single repeat course may be considered for women <34+0 weeks who received an initial course more than 7 days earlier and remain at high risk of imminent birth, per current NICE/RCOG guidance (as reported — verify exact wording against primary text before adoption). Maximum two courses. *[Certainty: guideline-consensus]*
- **Caution specifically where the pregnancy may end at term:** the clearest harm signal in the repeat-course literature (MACS-5 gestational-age-stratified analysis) is concentrated in babies who were ultimately born ≥37 weeks (OR 1.69, 95% CI 1.04–2.77), not in the overall repeat-course cohort (OR 1.02). *[Certainty: Moderate — refs 40–41]*
- Repeat dosing reduces birthweight, length and head circumference at birth (MACS: birthweight −114g, p=0.0026) without a corresponding reduction in short-term composite morbidity/mortality. *[Certainty: Moderate — ref 39]*
- Long-term follow-up of repeat-course RCT cohorts (ACTORDS, to 20.5 years) shows no clear cardiometabolic, bone, or neurocognitive harm signal, though confidence intervals remain wide. *[Certainty: Moderate — refs 43–44]*

## 9. Late preterm (34–36+6 weeks) policy

- ACS reduces the neonatal respiratory composite (RR 0.80, 95% CI 0.66–0.97) but increases neonatal hypoglycaemia (RR 1.60, 95% CI 1.37–1.87), mostly transient and resolving within 24 hours. *[Certainty: High for both effects — ref 45]*
- No adverse neurodevelopmental signal at a median of 7 years' follow-up. *[Certainty: Moderate-high — ref 46]*
- Real-world absolute benefit is smaller than the trial's efficacy estimate (NNT ≈46 real-world vs 35 in-trial) — counsel accordingly rather than quoting the trial's relative-risk reduction alone. *[Certainty: Moderate]*
- Ensure enhanced glucose monitoring is in place for late-preterm infants exposed to a completed ACS course, consistent with the ALPS hypoglycaemia signal. *[Certainty: High]*

## 10. Documentation and coding standards

For every ACS episode, document: agent(s) given, exact doses and times, total course status (complete/incomplete/mixed/repeat — and if repeat, number of prior courses and interval since the last), gestational age at each dose, interval from first dose to delivery, and presence/absence of chorioamnionitis at time of dosing. This dataset is the minimum needed to support both individual counselling and future unit audit.

## 11. Audit measures

Suggested unit audit metrics, drawn directly from the evidence gaps identified in this synthesis:
- Proportion of eligible women receiving at least one dose before delivery, stratified by gestational age.
- Proportion completing the full course, and interval from first dose to delivery, to locally validate against the continuous-benefit relationship described in §4.
- Rate of mixed-agent and incomplete courses, and whether local documentation meets the standard in §6/§10.
- Repeat-course rate and adherence to the ≥7-day, maximum-two-course policy.
- Late-preterm ACS use and associated neonatal hypoglycaemia rate, to locally benchmark against the ALPS trial figures.
- Any case in which chorioamnionitis was used as a reason to withhold ACS, for individual review against §5.
